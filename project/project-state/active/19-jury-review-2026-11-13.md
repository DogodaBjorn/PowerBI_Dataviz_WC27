## 3.1 · Analytical Quality — 8.0 / 10

*What a judge checks: accurate calculations, appropriate methods, correct interpretation, well-supported conclusions.*

### Where it hits the brief

- **The method fits the decision.** Every legal package (≥ 3 moves, ≥ 1 growth, within $60M, exclusions and
  dependencies respected) is computed and plotted: 456 packages, "only 6 pay back by 2032". That replaces opinion
  with an exhaustive search, which is rare in a contest entry.
- **The finance rules are honoured and stated:** 8 % discount, 2025 dollars, horizon 2032, "nothing after 2032 is
  counted" (P2 subtitle), "parts are rounded to add up".
- **Uncertainty is shown, not hidden:** Low/Expected/High toggle, NPV range on every package card, a Low-case
  warning line ("no package pays back by 2032; A ends at −$5.5M").
- **Interpretation is careful:** "An association, not proof of cause" (F3), "2021 is a depressed base year" (source
  line), "Our reading:" (F2) separates fact from opinion.
- **Unit economics in the folio table (F2):** $109.31 paid, $19.68 commission, $89.63 kept versus direct. Correct,
  concrete, verifiable.
- **The counterfactual is quantified:** "At the 2021 channel mix, 2025 commissions would be $4.8M lower."

### Where it misses or is open to challenge

| ID | Sev. | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| J-AQ-1 | Major | **The horizon drives the answer and the report half-owns it.** NPV through 2032 only penalises long-life assets (refresh, new hotel, extensions) by construction. The headline "$50.6M held back" can read as an artefact of the cut-off rather than a business conclusion. The subtitle states the rule; the page does not show what the long-life moves would look like on a run-rate or payback-after-2032 basis, so the Board cannot judge whether holding back 84 % of the budget is prudent or just an effect of the method |
| J-AQ-2 | Major | **Why Kiosks are in A is not argued on the page.** Kiosks carry −$0.6M NPV inside the "recommended" package. A judge will ask whether A is the NPV-maximising package or the best package that meets the three-move rule. The considered-B card implies the logic ("$2.25M more capex for $0.6M less NPV"), but the Recommended view never says that the third move is the least costly way to meet the rule, or that Kiosks depend on the app |
| J-AQ-3 | Minor | **F3 covers 44 of 50 hotels without saying so.** "The three bands hold 14, 20 and 10 hotels" = 44; the brand has 50. Six Goodnight Inn hotels have no renovation date in the data (two opened in 2022/2023). The exclusion is legitimate but unexplained; a judge who adds up the numbers will notice |
| J-AQ-4 | Minor | **The F3 headline and the KPI use different NPS framings.** The brand NPS is +3.3 and positive, the 10-year band is −4.9. That's consistent, but the band averages aren't stated as weighted or unweighted on the chart itself (the source line covers it for guest scores in general) |
| J-AQ-5 | Minor | **The 2032 run-rate is shown for A only** ("+$3.5M profit a year by 2032"). B, C and build-your-own report NPV and cash-positive year but not exit velocity, so the comparison B/C vs A uses one lens fewer than the recommendation |
| J-AQ-6 | Minor | **Risk is quantified only as scenario spread.** No visible treatment of execution or dependency risk (GI-02 depends on GI-01; what happens to Kiosks if the app slips), adoption risk for the app, or rooms out of service during works. The brief says "explain … risks" |

## 3.2 · Insightfulness — 7.5 / 10

*What a judge checks: relevance of insights, how well the two questions are answered, usefulness for decisions.*

### Where it hits the brief

- **P1 headline is a complete diagnosis in one sentence:** "Full rooms, a slipping margin: Goodnight Inn pays the
  middleman, and its welcome is fading." The Board has the story before reading a chart.
- **KPI row with peer context:** every KPI says where Goodnight Inn ranks among the four brands and which direction
  it is moving ("best of 4", "2nd of 4, −2.8 pt since 2023", "highest of 4", "last of 4").
- **The insights connect:** full rooms (occupancy best of 4) + commission leakage (OTA share 33 % → 50 %) = margin
  falling despite demand. That's a real "so what", not a metric tour.
- **P2 headline is a decision:** "Fund one package of three moves: $9.4M now, $50.6M held back."
- **The considered alternatives answer the Board's obvious objections**, especially "C · Why not renovate?", which
  closes the loop from F3.
- **A trigger for the held-back money exists** ("Revisit once A has broken even, or if NPS falls below 0") on the
  C card.

### Where it misses or is open to challenge

| ID | Sev. | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| J-IN-1 | **Major (rule)** | **No operational impact through 2032.** The brief asks for financial *and operational* impact. P2 speaks only in dollars. The Board doesn't see what A does to the problems P1 raised: OTA share, margin after commissions, NPS/guest score, occupancy, by 2032. The story that P1 opens (middleman, fading welcome) is never closed in operational terms |
| J-IN-2 | Major | **F4 ("Where we are full") ends nowhere in the recommendation.** The airport finding links to the +40-room extensions and the Boston hotel, none of which are recommended. On the Recommended view the rec note says A answers "F2, F3, F1". A Board member will ask why a quarter of P1 was spent on a finding that does not drive the decision, or what the decision about it is |
| J-IN-3 | Major | **F3 → Kiosks is a weak causal bridge.** F3 shows that renovation age predicts NPS. The recommended response to F3 is kiosks at 20 desks, not renovation. P1 has no evidence that check-in or the desk experience is what drives the fading welcome (complaint categories, for example). A judge may see the recommendation as answering a different question than the finding asked |
| J-IN-4 | Minor | **The fate of $50.6M is a sub-clause.** Holding back 84 % of the budget is the boldest call on the page, but the Recommended view explains it in one sentence in the rec note; the decision rule for releasing it only appears in the Considered view |
| J-IN-5 | Minor | **Growth initiative not called out.** The rule requirement (≥ 1 growth) is met but invisible on the Recommended view. A judge checking compliance has to open Build your own to confirm it |
| J-IN-6 | Minor | **The F4 insight is descriptive.** "The airports are the fullest at 82.0 %" states a fact; the implication (capacity constraint? pricing headroom? where to add rooms?) is left for the reader |

## 3.3 · Visual Effectiveness — 7.0 / 10

*What a judge checks: layout, navigation, text, colours, choice of visuals.*

### Where it hits the brief

- **A clear reading path:** headline → KPI row → three finding cards → a full-width F4 row on P1; hero scatter on
  the left, recommendation on the right on P2. Same card grid on both pages.
- **Action titles on every chart**, so each chart states its finding ("After commissions, the margin fell 2.8
  points since 2023").
- **Disciplined palette:** Goodnight blue for the brand / recommendation, grey for peers and "other", orange for
  negative or warning. Used consistently across pages.
- **Direct labels instead of legends** in F1 ("Before commissions" / "After commissions") and on F3/F4 values.
- **The scatter carries a "How to read this chart" guide** with a legend and the payback zone shaded and labelled.
- **Navigation is native and legible:** three state tabs and a Low/Expected/High pill row, with the active state
  filled.

### Where it misses or is open to challenge

| ID | Sev. | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| J-VE-1 | **Major** | **Locale leakage in the render.** In Desktop (nl-NL), the waterfall's total bar reads **"Totaal"** and data labels use decimal commas ("+6,8", "−4,9", "91,9%", "+$3,4M", "−$0,6M"), while cards and titles on the same screen use points ("79.4%", "+$3.3M"). Any screenshot or submission image taken from this machine shows mixed number formats and a Dutch word in an English report. What an en-US judge sees in the PBIX or Publish to Web has not been verified |
| J-VE-2 | Major | **F4's columns don't encode anything visible.** The seven hotels range from 85.2 % to 91.9 %, but the bars render as near-identical blocks; the information is carried only by the labels. A judge reads this as a chart used as a label strip. Hotel names also wrap to two lines under columns |
| J-VE-3 | Minor | **The F2 line chart's key value isn't on the chart.** The title says 50 %; the axis shows only 20 % and 40 % and the line ends above the top gridline without a label |
| J-VE-4 | Minor | **The hero scatter spends most of its area on the losers.** About 450 grey dots fill the plot; the decision-relevant region (A and B, top left) is a small corner where A and B overlap. The zoom state exists but is a second click away |
| J-VE-5 | Minor | **The waterfall is visually weak in the Recommended view:** short bars in a tall visual; Kiosks is a sliver; labels in a different number format from the rec tile above (J-VE-1) |
| J-VE-6 | Minor | **The cumulative chart legend is hard to decode:** "Low ····· Expected — High — Case in view" (four entries, where "Expected" and "Case in view" draw on top of each other). Line style carries scenario, colour carries "in view" |
| J-VE-7 | Minor | **Mixed unit notation in the rec tile:** "$9.4M", "+$3.3M", then "−5.5 to +12.7" with the unit in the label ("NPV range, $M"). Same in B/C cards |
| J-VE-8 | Minor | **Orange does two jobs:** it means "negative / warning" (F3 bar, Kiosks, Low case, over budget) and also marks the interaction cue (the ⓘ in "Hover a bar", "Revisit the facts", "Definitions"). Six orange cues per page compete with the orange data |
| J-VE-9 | Minor | **P2 density.** The Recommended view has a title, a guide box, a scatter, tabs, scenario pills, a rec tile with four KPIs and a note, a waterfall, a cumulative chart, a warning line and a footer row with seven interactive zones. It's well organised, but a Board member needs time on it; the footer row's text is the smallest on the page |

## 3.4 · Creativity & Innovation — 8.5 / 10

*What a judge checks: creativity, design, storytelling, innovative use of Power BI.*

### Where it hits the brief

- **A novel analytical angle**, the thing past judges rewarded: plotting the entire legal decision space (456
  packages) instead of three hand-picked options. I would not expect another entry to have it.
- **Three states on one page** (Recommended / Considered / Build your own) solve the two-page limit without
  drillthrough, and turn the Board from readers into participants.
- **Build your own** with a live budget alarm, its own waterfall and a comparison against A is a genuine
  decision tool, not a gimmick.
- **Tooltip pages as "the page in miniature"** (package card with range bar and move table, year tooltip comparing
  A/B/C/your plan) add depth without leaving the page.
- **Dynamic alt text driven by measures** is innovative use of Power BI accessibility features.
- **A brand voice** (the hotel puns in the eyebrows: "The wake-up call", "Room to invest", "Where we are full")
  gives personality without hurting clarity.

### Where it misses or is open to challenge

| ID | Sev. | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| J-CI-1 | Minor | **Risk of "over-engineered".** P2 holds 79 visuals, layered scatters, bookmark states, a Deneb spec and seven hover zones in the footer. Judges have explicitly praised designs that "feel native to Power BI without being over-engineered". The craft is visible; the effort to *use* it may count against it |
| J-CI-2 | Minor | **The best innovation is hidden.** Build your own and the considered alternatives sit behind tabs; a judge who scores from the submission image or the first screen only sees the Recommended view |
| J-CI-3 | Minor | **Storytelling ends at P2's headline.** P1 builds a strong narrative tension; P2 doesn't close it in the same voice (no "and this is what happens to the margin / the middleman / the welcome by 2032"). See J-IN-1 |

## 3.5 · Accessibility — 7.0 / 10

*What a judge checks: alt text, logical tab order, sufficient contrast (and, in practice, keyboard and
colour-blind use).*

### Where it hits the brief

- **Contrast measured on the 13 Nov render, all text samples well above 4.5:1:** body/axis grey #4B5466 on white
  7.6:1, on the light blue guide 6.7:1, on the footer pills 6.3:1; orange context text #A63F0E 6.3:1; blue context
  text #1763A8 6.2:1; tags and guide text 13 to 16:1. White on Tangerine was explicitly avoided (3.4:1).
- **Alt text on every chart, driven by measures** (Alt F1 Chart, Alt Scatter, Alt Waterfall, Alt Cumulative and so
  on), so the description follows the selected scenario/state. The Deneb selector has a descriptive literal alt text.
- **Explicit tab order** on both pages, in reading order (header → KPIs → F1 → F2 → F3 → F4 on P1). The project's
  `a11y_check` reports 0 errors.
- **Not colour alone:** A/B/C are lettered markers, negative values carry a minus sign as well as orange, lines are
  labelled directly. The blue/orange/grey palette is colour-blind safe.
- **Power BI's default tooltips are switched off** where they would show off-brand raw field names.

### Where it misses or is open to challenge

| ID | Sev. | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| J-AC-1 | **Major** | **Meaning lives behind hover.** The F3 band detail, the F4 hotel detail, the package cards, the year comparison, the four findings on P2 ("Revisit the facts" is the *only* place P2 shows them) and the Definitions are tooltip-only. Accessibility guidance says tooltip content must also exist as static text. A keyboard, touch or screen-reader user misses it |
| J-AC-2 | Major | **The cues address mouse users only:** "Hover a bar / a hotel / a dot / a year". Keyboard and touch behaviour of the report-page tooltips and of the footer chip zones has not been tested (doc 18 open item; the bridge cannot open tooltips) |
| J-AC-3 | Major | **The Deneb build selector's keyboard operability is unknown.** "Tap a move to add or remove it" — if the Vega selection only responds to the pointer, the Build your own state is not usable without a mouse |
| J-AC-4 | Minor | **Three scatter overlay visuals have no alt text** (`hero_packages_hover`, `hero_s_packages_hover`, `hero_s_packages_build`). If they're reachable by the screen reader or the tab order, they announce as unnamed charts; P2 also stacks several scatters for one visible chart |
| J-AC-5 | Minor | **Tab order on P2 reaches the hero scatter before the state tabs and the scenario pills**, so a keyboard user meets the chart before the controls that change it. Defensible as left-to-right order, but worth a judge's keyboard pass |
| J-AC-6 | Minor | **Smallest text is small at typical viewing size.** Source lines, chart legends, the footer pill row and the scatter guide are about 12 px at 1920 × 1080; judges often view at "fit to page" on a laptop, which makes them about 9 px |
| J-AC-7 | Minor | **Mixed decimal separators** (J-VE-1) also hurt screen-reader output and readability for low-vision users who rely on consistent number formats |

## 4 · Cross-cutting observations for the merge

1. **The two strongest assets (the package scatter and P1's diagnosis) are not tied together on P2 in operational
   terms.** J-IN-1, J-IN-2, J-IN-3 and J-CI-3 are the same gap seen from four criteria: the P1 problems never come
   back as 2032 outcomes.
2. **Hover is carrying too much.** J-AC-1, J-AC-2, J-IN-4 and J-CI-2 all point at content a judge only meets if
   they hover in the right place.
3. **The environment changes what the judge sees.** J-VE-1 (nl-NL locale) and the open Publish-to-Web gate mean the
   scored artefact may differ from what was reviewed in Desktop here.
4. **Small evidence gaps a numerate judge will find:** J-AQ-2 (why Kiosks), J-AQ-3 (44 of 50 hotels), J-AQ-1 (the
   horizon artefact).
