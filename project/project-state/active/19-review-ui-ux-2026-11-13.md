## Verdict

**Beautiful: yes.** The two pages look like one designed object. There is a strong Night masthead, a calm warm
canvas, one typeface, white cards with the same anatomy, action titles that tell the story on their own, and no chart
junk. A juror scrolling through 50 entries will stop on this one.

**Useful: yes, for a reader who reads.** Every card answers a question and the second page turns the findings into a
decision. The weak spots are not in the content. They are in **invitation and wayfinding**:

1. Nothing on the canvas takes you from page 1 to page 2, or back.
2. The strongest interactive feature (Build your own) sits behind a neutral third tab.
3. Tangerine means too many things, and one of those meanings ("your plan") clashes with the meaning page 1 taught
   ("problem").
4. The controls on page 2 speak three visual languages, and the two page-level controls look like they belong to
   the right-hand card only.

All of these can be fixed before 17 Nov without touching the story.

## Keep (do not "fix" these)

- **Action titles** on every card and on both pages: "Fund one package of three moves: $9.4M now, $50.6M held back."
  is exactly what a decision page should say.
- **Card anatomy on P1:** code · name / headline / chart / note, the same in F1 to F3, and F4 follows the same pattern.
  It reads like a well-set newspaper page.
- **Grey = context, blue = focus**, held across both pages and the tooltips (other brands, "before commissions", other
  packages, High case: all grey).
- **The hover rule from doc 18:** the cue sits on the thing you point at and says the verb ("Hover a bar"). That
  removed the legend problem; the principle is right.
- **Definitions tooltip:** a nine-term glossary is a real gift to a non-finance jury.
- **The P2 tab bar does not move between states** (same x/y in all three bookmarks), so the button you clicked stays
  under your cursor.
- **The tooltip skin** (orange band plus white card) is distinctive and consistent across all nine pages.
- **Hygiene:** filter pane hidden (`outspacePane.visible false`), Power BI's default tooltip off wherever no page tooltip
  is bound, alt text on hover targets.

## Findings in detail

### A. Wayfinding

**UX-01 (S1): No route between the pages on the canvas.**

- *What I see:* P1 ends with the source line and Definitions. There is no "next", nothing points at the investment
  page, and the eyebrow says "THE WAKE-UP CALL · THE BRAND REVIEW" with no page count. P2 has no "back" either.
  "Revisit the facts" sounds like a link to P1, but it is hover-only.
- *Why it matters:* in Publish to Web, page switching lives in the embed's grey bottom bar. A juror who reads P1 on
  its own reads half the argument and never sees the recommendation. Second-page drop-off is the most common
  failure of two-page entries.
- *Recommendation:*
  - P1, bottom right on the footer row, left of Definitions: a page-navigation button "Next: the investment
    strategy →" (Power BI button, action Page navigation; it works under Publish to Web).
  - P2: make the "Revisit the facts" label (not the F-zones, which keep their hover) navigate to P1, or add
    "← The Brand Review" next to the eyebrow.
  - Add the count to both eyebrows: "1 / 2 · THE BRAND REVIEW", "2 / 2 · THE INVESTMENT STRATEGY".
- *Conflict check:* none with earlier decisions.

### B. Colour semantics

**UX-02 (S1): Tangerine is overloaded.**

- *What I see:* Tangerine (and Burnt Tangerine) stands for:
  1. bad news: the KPI sublines, the −4.9 bar, the Low-case note, the over-budget band, negative NPV in the selector,
     the Kiosks bar;
  2. "hover here": the tags, the Definitions and facts pills;
  3. the finding codes F1 to F4;
  4. the Low case (dashed line, rec);
  5. package C (dashed line, Considered);
  6. **your plan** (dot and line, Build).
- *Why it matters:* P1 teaches "orange = something is wrong". On P2 the reader builds a plan and it turns orange,
  which reads as "your plan is a problem" even when it beats A. Codes, hover cues and warnings in one colour also blur
  the three roles: the eye can't tell what's clickable from what's bad.
- *Recommendation:* keep Tangerine for **warning + hover** only (the doc 18 logic: the tag shows the tooltip's
  colour).
  - **Your plan:** give it its own mark that doesn't depend on colour: a Night line plus a hollow ring dot, or
    Coffee Blue dashed.
  - **F-codes:** set them in Night/Dusk semibold. They are labels, not alarms.
  - **Low case:** grey dashed is enough; the Low-case sentence underneath already carries the warning.
  - **C:** keep orange (C is the bad option) or use grey dashed.
- *Conflict check:* the F-code colour comes from the P1 kickers (earlier rounds). Bjorn's call.

### C. Controls

**UX-03 (S2): Three button languages, and the hierarchy is inverted.**

- *What I see:*
  - *All packages / Zoom on A, B, C:* rounded pill, Night fill. A secondary view switch, yet the heaviest-looking
    control on the page.
  - *Recommended: A / Considered: B and C / Build your own:* square rectangles, blue fill when on, thin outline when
    off. These switch the whole page, yet the off states look like ghost buttons.
  - *Low / Expected / High:* pills, Night fill (same as the zoom, different from the tabs).
  - The selector uses circles for multi-select (see UX-05).
- *Why it matters:* consistency is how a reader learns "this is a control". Here the most important switch looks the
  plainest.
- *Recommendation:* one segmented-control component (one radius, one "on" fill, one outline) in two sizes:
  - **large** for the story tabs: first, full width of the control row, "on" in Night or blue, consistently;
  - **small** for the scenario and the zoom.

  All three already sit on 40 px rows, so this is restyling, not re-layout.

**UX-04 (S2): Page-level controls live inside the right card.**

- *What I see:* the tab bar changes both columns (the scatter shrinks, the comparison chart appears below). The
  scenario slicer changes "every number on this page" (its own alt text), including the scatter and the subtitle
  ("Expected case."). Both sit at the top of the right card (`tab_*` x 1104, `sl_scenario` x 1200).
- *Why it matters:* readers infer scope from containment. A control inside a card controls that card. Changing Low →
  High and seeing the scatter move is a surprise, not a confirmation.
- *Recommendation:* one control row spanning the page between masthead and cards: story tabs left, scenario right,
  with the word "Scenario". If layout room is too tight before 17 Nov, at least put the scenario next to the subtitle,
  which already names the case, and leave the tabs where they are.

### D. Invitation to explore

**UX-05 (S2): Build your own is the best feature and the least invited.**

- *What I see:*
  - (a) It is the third tab with a neutral label. Nothing in the recommended state suggests trying it; only the
    year tooltip says "Your plan: none yet", and you see that only on hover.
  - (b) The empty state shows "$0.0M / $0.0M / +0.0 to +0.0", a ghost waterfall with "$0,0M Totaal", and "Nothing
    picked yet" twice (the tile title and the selector footer).
  - (c) The selector rows use **radio-style circles** for a multi-select, which reads as "pick one".
  - (d) The instruction says "Tap"; most jurors use a mouse.
- *Why it matters:* juries reward interactivity that invites. An empty state full of zeros looks broken, and radio
  circles make a reader stop after one click.
- *Recommendation:*
  - (a) One line in the recommendation tile or under the tab bar: "Think you can beat A? Build your own →" (button to
    the bookmark).
  - (b) Hide the numbers and the waterfall until the first pick; show the rules plus one sentence. Optionally add a
    "Start from A" preset.
  - (c) Square checkboxes.
  - (d) "Click" or "Pick".
- *Note:* the Deneb selector has no keyboard support (known, for the submission notes).

**UX-16 (S3, watch): Hover tags look like buttons.**

A filled pill with an icon is the platform's button convention, so some readers will click "Hover a bar". Nothing
breaks (the tag is decorative), and the verb corrects them. "Hover" also doesn't apply on touch (tap opens the
tooltip in the service). Bjorn approved this design on 12 Nov. Watch it in any user test; no change proposed.

### E. Chart clarity

**UX-06 (S2): The scatter's legend does not match its marks, and the answer hides in a corner.**

- *What I see (zoom 04, top):*
  - The guide's key shows "● A", "● B and C", "○ every other package". The real marks are **lettered discs** (A blue,
    B/C Night) and **filled** grey dots. The hollow ring doesn't exist on the chart.
  - The guide panel (`#EAF2FB`) and the "Pays back by 2032" band are the same light blue, 16 px apart, so they read as
    one block.
  - A and B sit in a ~40 px corner at the top left, B touching a grey dot.
- *Recommendation:*
  - Make the key glyphs the actual marks (a small lettered disc, a filled grey dot).
  - Give the guide a neutral tint (Ice or the card's cream) or just a hairline. That also clears the known pbir_detect
    `#EAF2FB` finding.
  - In the "All packages" view, annotate A directly ("A · $9.4M, pays back") so the answer is visible without the
    zoom.

**UX-07 (S2): The cumulative chart (recommended state) has four legend keys for three visible lines.**

- *What I see (zoom 04, bottom):*
  - "Low ---- Expected ······ High —— Case in view ——". The dotted Expected line lies under the blue "Case in view"
    line, so one key points at nothing.
  - "Case in view" is a builder's term, not a reader's.
  - The y axis shows +$20M and $0M only, while the Low line runs below zero without a tick.
- *Recommendation:* draw the three cases and highlight the selected one (thicker, blue), with direct labels at the
  right end (the way F1 does it). Drop the "Case in view" key and add a −$10M tick.

**UX-08 (S2): The P1 F4 bars carry no information.**

- *What I see (zoom 04, middle):* seven columns about 40 px tall, from zero, for 85.2 to 91.9%. They are equal stubs,
  so the eye reads seven identical tiles. The claim ("5 of 7 are airports") rests on colour and labels, and blue vs
  grey has no key (blue means "airport" only on this card).
- *Recommendation:* a ranked strip of seven labelled tiles or chips, airports marked (blue + "Airport"), values as text,
  no bars. Or a dot plot on an 84-93% axis (honest for dots, not for bars). Add a two-item key or put "airport" in the
  colour sentence of the title.

**UX-13 (S3): The small scatter (Considered / Build) uses different markers.**

A/B/C are squares under the dots ("A B" crowded), while the large scatter uses lettered discs. In Build the orange plan
dot covers B's label (FinalV2 05). *Recommendation:* the same lettered discs as the large scatter; the plan dot as a
ring with a "You" label offset to the right.

**UX-15 (S3): The F2 card is cramped.**

The line chart is ~100 px with two y ticks (20%, 40%), and the "20%" tick sits on the x axis next to "2021". The table
rows are tight, while F1 next to it has spare height. *Recommendation:* drop the y axis and label start/end values on
the lines (33% → 50%, matching the title), or give the chart 32-40 px from the note.

### F. Craft and neatness

**UX-09 (S3): The footer "Revisit the facts" pill.**

- The pill tint (`#FCE6DB`) on the warm gradient footer is roughly 1.1:1, so it barely reads as a chip, especially at
  the right where the canvas turns peach.
- The separator dots hug the next code ("· F2") instead of sitting centred in the gap.
- The names sit 2-3 px below their codes. Bjorn accepted this on 12 Nov; I would still fix it if it's a 2 px padding
  change.
- *Recommendation:* white fill or a hairline border in Burnt Tangerine at ~30% (also for Definitions), centred
  separators, top padding 4 → 2 px.

**UX-10 (S3): The recommendation tile.**

- It is the only card-in-card with a blue outline and an outer glow, so it reads as a focused or selected state rather
  than "our answer".
- "−5.5 to +12.7" carries its unit in the label ("NPV range, $M"), while the three neighbours carry $ and M in the
  number.
- *Recommendation:* the same flat card treatment as the B/C cards (they also have a header icon), with emphasis from
  the title rather than the border. Write "−$5.5M to +$12.7M" if it fits; otherwise keep the convention but apply it to
  the B, C and plan tiles too (it already is, so the fix is just dropping the glow).

**UX-12 (S3): The Considered cards (FinalV2 03).**

- B's title is a statement ("Breakfast instead of kiosks") and C's is a question ("Why not renovate?").
- The text sits in fixed slots, which leaves a ~70 px hole between paragraphs in B.
- The grey key icon looks like a placeholder.
- *Recommendation:* the same headline grammar, paragraphs stacked with one fixed gap, and the icon given a meaning
  (the package letter) or removed.

### G. Copy and notation (UI copy only)

**UX-11 (S3): Precision and format change between neighbours.**

- "+$3.4M" in the selector table and "+$3,4M" on the waterfall right next to it.
- "$3.28M / $9.40M" in the package tooltip against "$3.3M / $9.4M" on the page.
- "Totaal".

The locale part is decision M3 (accepted: en-US in an en-US browser). The **precision** part is not locale and is
cheap: one rule (one decimal in $M everywhere, two only below $1M). For the jury's browser locale see the Publish to
Web check below. If the jury uses nl/de/fr browsers, the native data labels will mix with the text measures on every
card.

### H. Tooltips

**UX-14 (S3): Two tooltips are weaker than the cards they explain.**

- *tt-package:* the range strip ("| break-even", the letters C / A / B stacked on hair ticks, "Low −5.5" and
  "High +12.7" at the ends with no axis) needs decoding. The card has ~100 px of empty space at the bottom, and the
  moves table uses a tighter, smaller type than every other text block.
  *Recommendation:* replace the strip with one sentence ("Low −$5.5M · Expected +$3.3M · High +$12.7M; break-even
  is 0") or a labelled range bar with three ticks; trim the page height; set the table at the body size.
- *tt-f3:* the bars are 10-20 px tall inside a ~200 px plot area, so the tooltip shows the chart smaller than the P1
  card. *Recommendation:* taller bars, or just the three numbers.

## Check in Publish to Web (UX angle, add to the M4 gate)

1. **Visual headers.** Data visuals keep the visual header on (on purpose, for "show as table"). In the service,
   hovering F3, F4 or the cumulative chart shows the header icons at the visual's top right, which is where the
   hover tags sit. Check for overlap; if they collide, move the tag left of the icon zone.
2. **Tooltip scale at 1366 px.** The 480 px tooltip pages scale with the canvas (`useScaledTooltips`). At a 1366 px
   browser width, 16 px text becomes about 11 px. Check that the glossary and the package table are still readable.
3. **Page switching.** Where the embed puts the page control (bottom bar, arrows or tabs), and whether a first-time
   reader finds it. This is the baseline for UX-01.
4. **Click-through.** Clicking a hover tag, a footer pill zone or a card: no selection outline or dimming of other
   visuals that looks like a bug.
5. **Browser locale.** One pass in an nl or de browser to see the notation mix (UX-11) the way a European juror
   would.
6. **Touch.** One tap test on a tablet: do the tooltips open, and can the selector be used?
