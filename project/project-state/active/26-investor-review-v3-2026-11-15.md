## 1.3 Criterion by criterion

### Thesis and the ask: 9.0

The page 2 headline is what a committee wants on the first line of a paper: "Fix the middleman first: $9.4M now earns
$3.5M a year by 2032" (`texts/05`). The subtitle says what we are not deciding today ("The rest waits for one Board
meeting in mid-2028") and why the page shows a second measure next to NPV ("NPV counts nothing after 2032"). The
reason for now is on the recommendation card: "Every year without it leaves about $2.4M with the agencies"
(`texts/05`). The headline follows the scenario pill: $1.0M in Low (`texts/06`), $6.2M in High (`texts/07`). That
is honest, and it still reads as an ask in all three.

Page 1 sets it up with one sentence the Board can repeat: "Full rooms, a slipping margin: Goodnight Inn pays the
middleman, and its welcome is fading." (`texts/01`). Short of a 10 only because of 1.3.2: the headline's "middleman"
is the smaller part of the margin story.

### Diagnosis and evidence: 8.5

Strong. Every KPI on page 1 is ranked against the three other brands ("best of 4", "2nd of 4", "highest of 4",
"last of 4", `texts/01`). F2's room-night table is the best single piece of evidence in the entry: via an agency the
inn keeps $89.63, direct $107.10, "the guest pays $2.25 more and we keep $17.47 more" (`texts/01`). F3 says
"an association, not proof of cause" in plain words. F4 turns occupancy into a demand test: "Toronto topped 90% in
17 of the last 24 months, Gatwick in 14, Montréal in 13; Kingston, the third extension on offer, in none". The
tooltips add depth where a director would probe: the oldest band's 10 hotels and 1,215 rooms, its RevPAR and its
weakest guest scores (`02`); Toronto's month-by-month line (`03`). The Definitions chip covers every term a director
could stumble on (`04`).

The gap is one of emphasis. F1 says "Commissions took 1.0 of those points and running costs 1.8 (staff rose from
26.9% to 28.3% of revenue)" (`texts/01`). So the larger cause of the margin fall is running costs, not the middleman.
The title says "Goodnight Inn pays the middleman", and page 2 says "Fix the middleman first". A director who reads
F1's note will ask why the plan leads with the smaller cause. The answer exists (the agency share is the trend that
keeps growing, and the app is the best move on the list), but the page does not give it. See INV3-04.

### Capital allocation and returns: 7.5

What convinces. The hero table ranks the ten moves by yield on cost with NPV beside it and draws both hurdles: "8%:
our cost of money" and "pays back by 2032" (`05`). The title says plainly which moves fail which test: "Two moves pass
both tests; the kiosks earn 20.6% a year on cost but pay back after 2032". What waits shows that all 456 packages
were tried and that "None of the 456 packages beats A through 2032" (`09`). That is the discipline I want: not "we
picked three good ideas" but "we tried every combination". The capital plan accounts for every dollar: $9.4M +
$12.4M + $29.5M + $8.7M = $60.0M, and the $8.7M "stays with the group" (`texts/05`). Returning unneeded capital is
the right instinct.

What does not. Three things a CFO would find in ten minutes.

1. **The breakfast upgrade (INV3-01, Major).** It costs $4.3M (`texts/16`), earns 19.8% a year on cost and has the
   least negative NPV of all the moves that wait, −$1.1M (`texts/05`). It ranks above Gatwick (15.8%, −$3.5M) and
   Toronto (11.5%, −$3.7M) on both tests, and far above the refresh (7.9%, −$27.3M). Yet Gatwick, Toronto and the
   refresh get a place at the 2028 gate and breakfast gets "not now" with no date. The caption's reason, "Not now:
   Energy and Boston exceed the $8.7M left", does not cover breakfast: $4.3M fits in the $8.7M the plan hands back.
   What waits explains breakfast only as a swap for the kiosks (package B, "Not planned", `texts/09`), never as an
   addition. A move that answers F3 in all 50 hotels, clears our 8% twice over and fits in the money we return is
   the first thing the committee will ask about.
2. **A against B on the page's own second lens (INV3-02, Major).** The subtitle says NPV stops at 2032, "so we also
   show what each move earns a year once live". On that lens B beats A in every case: $4.0M against $3.5M a year
   (Expected), $1.3M against $1.0M (Low), $6.7M against $6.2M (High) (`texts/09`, `10`, `11`, `05`). The extra
   ~$0.5M a year costs $2.3M more, about 20% a year on the extra money. The cumulative cash is level in Expected
   (both +$6.8M) and B is ahead in High (+$19.7M against +$19.2M). The "Why not now" cell argues only on NPV ("$2.3M
   more than A for $0.6M less through 2032") and the queues. A's better downside (Low −$5.5M against B's −$6.9M) is
   a good reason to prefer A, but the page has to say it is choosing downside over run-rate. Today it applies its
   second lens everywhere except to the one choice where that lens disagrees.
3. **The refresh holds the biggest tranche (INV3-03, Major).** $29.5M, half the budget, is reserved for the move with
   the lowest yield on the list, 7.9% (4.5% in Low), and an NPV of −$27.3M through 2032 (`texts/05`, `06`). The page
   is candid ("just under our 8%. The data cannot price the cost of waiting, so this case is understated", `texts/09`)
   and the gate only lets it "return with a case beyond 2032". That is fair. But the capital plan's title, "$41.9M at
   the 2028 gate", reads as an allocation, and nowhere does the page add up what the whole plan earns if every tranche
   is released. From the plan bar: $3.5M + $1.7M + $2.3M = $7.5M a year on $51.3M, about 14.6% (`texts/05`). That one
   line, and the NPV beside it, belongs on the page. See INV3-05.

A credibility detail: the Build view lists A's three moves at +$2.4M, +$0.8M and +$0.4M a year (`texts/16`), which add
to $3.6M, while every headline says $3.5M. The footer says "Parts are rounded to add up" (`texts/05`). The data gives
$2.35M + $0.77M + $0.41M = $3.53M (`project_economics.csv`): the app rounds up and the total rounds down. A CFO who
adds the column will notice. See INV3-07.

### Growth and value creation: 8.0

Every move carries "growth" or not in the Answers column (`05`), every tranche shows "a year once live" and yield on
cost, and the Build view checks the contest rule live ("Within the rules: 3 moves, 2 growth", `texts/16`). The
extensions are argued from demand (the 90% months), and Kingston is excluded on the same evidence ("Kingston (61%
full) is not on the list", `texts/09`). Good discipline: growth where the rooms are full, not everywhere.

What is missing is the 2032 picture of the brand. The tracking table shows the brand in 2032 "with A" only: margin
35.1%, distribution cost 10.1% (`texts/05`). Nassetta's question is "how much bigger and more valuable is this brand
in 2032 if the plan runs as you hope?" The answer would be A plus both tranches: +80 rooms, about $7.5M a year, a
margin above 35.1%. The page never says it. See INV3-05.

### Risk, downside and governance: 8.5

This is now a strength. The scenario pill drives every number on the page ("every number on this page follows it",
aria in `texts/05`), and the Low case is shown without flinching: "In the Low case no move pays back by 2032; package A
still loses least of all 456 packages" (`texts/06`). The worst case sits on the recommendation card in the Expected
view: "Worst case (Low): −$5.5M through 2032, and still +$1.0M a year by 2032". The cumulative cash chart shows all
three cases with the gate marked, and the milestones under it say when each move goes live and when A turns cash
positive (`05`). The gate has a written rule: tranche 2 is released "if the first money worked (distribution cost
down 0.6 points or more, the Expected track by then) and Gatwick and Toronto topped 90% in 6 of the last 12 months.
… A rule, not a forecast." (`texts/05`). The 0.6 checks out: Expected's full effect is 1.2 points (11.3% → 10.1%), and
at the 50% ramp of the first full year that is 0.6.

Two gaps.
- **Tranche 3 has no measurable rule (INV3-06, Major).** "The refresh returns with a case beyond 2032 and the oldest
  hotels' NPS" names an input, not a threshold. $29.5M deserves a number: for example, the oldest band's NPS below
  a stated level, or a priced case above 8%.
- **The rooms half of the gate is already met (INV3-08, Minor).** Toronto topped 90% in 17 of the last 24 months and
  Gatwick in 14 (`texts/01`). The test is 6 of the last 12. Unless demand falls, the rooms test is a formality, so
  the gate really tests the app. That is a defensible rule (release more money once the first money has worked), but
  then say so. It also means the most robust move in the Low case, Gatwick at 13.3% (`texts/06`, above the app's
  12.1%), waits on an app metric it does not depend on.

### Accountability: 8.0

The tracking table is what I asked for in principle: per finding, the measure, the 2025 value, the 2032 value with
A, and a status in words ("half the rise undone", "half the fall recovered") (`texts/05`). It moves with the
scenario ("a quarter of the rise undone" in Low, `texts/06`). The milestones give dates: revenue management Jul 2027,
the app Oct 2027, kiosks Apr 2028 (`texts/05`). The gate gives a 2028 checkpoint.

What a committee would still hold the presenter to and cannot:
- **NPS has no target (INV3-09, Minor to Major).** F3's row says "not in A". The brand is "last of 4" and "falling
  every year" (`texts/01`), and nothing in the first $9.4M aims at it. That is a choice, but the board needs a
  number to watch while it waits: where must NPS be by the 2028 gate?
- **The running-cost half of the margin has no owner (INV3-04, Major).** "Half the fall recovered" means the other
  half, 1.4 points, stays. F1 puts running costs at 1.8 of the 2.8 points (`texts/01`); staff went from 26.9% to
  28.3% of revenue, about $3M a year on $212.8M (`gi.txt`). The plan's answer is the kiosks at +$0.4M a year
  (`texts/16`). Who owns the rest, and by when?

## 1.5 Experience

**Convincing?** Yes. Page 1 reads in a minute and every claim has its peer number beside it. Page 2 makes the ask in
the first line and backs it with a full search, two hurdles, three cases and a dated gate. That is more rigour than
most capital papers I have read.

**Enjoyable?** Page 1: 9.0. Calm, ranked, and the room-night table is a pleasure. Page 2: 8.5. Recommended is dense,
but every number earns its place; What waits answers the "why not" questions in one table; Build your own is the
part a director plays with, and it behaves (over budget, empty state, live rules check). At 1366 px the text gets
small (`22`).

## 1.6 Verdict on this version alone

**Would I invest in the person?** Yes. This is someone who tries every option, shows the downside first and writes a
rule before asking for more money.

**Would I fund tranche 1?** Yes. $9.4M for package A, now. A's case holds in Expected and High and loses least of all
456 packages in Low. The app alone carries it, and the reason for now ($2.4M a year to the agencies) is real.

**Would I approve the $60M plan?** Yes, with three conditions before the 2028 gate:
1. Explain breakfast or put it in the plan. It fits in the money returned and beats every move that waits (INV3-01).
2. Give tranche 3 a measurable rule and show what the whole plan earns if every gate opens (INV3-05, INV3-06).
3. Name an owner and a 2028 target for the running-cost half of the margin, and for NPS (INV3-04, INV3-09).

INV3-02 (A against B on run-rate) is one sentence; I would want it said in the room, not necessarily on the page.

  group", the complaint clause). I would rather have them than the white space.
- **"Not doing" on GR-2, DE-3, GR-4, RK-3: holds.** On GR-3 it holds for a 2032 brand panel, not for one line
  (INV3-05).
