## Verdict

**A big step.** FinalV2 was beautiful but easy to get lost in. FinalV3 is beautiful **and** you find your way:

- page count in the eyebrow, a Next button, a back button;
- every control in one row in the masthead;
- Build your own invited and opening on a real plan;
- orange back to meaning "warning";
- en-US numbers in every browser.

Thirteen of the sixteen earlier findings are fixed and three are partly fixed. None got worse.

**What's left is about density, not direction.** Page 2 opens on its densest view: two tables, a stacked bar and a
chart, with a lot of 15-16 px text. At a 1366 px laptop width (the jury's likely screen) that text renders at about
10 px, and the tracking table in the recommendation tile at about 9 px. Page 2 also reads more like an investment memo
than a dataviz page; for a Board that's right, for a dataviz jury it's a small loss of visual calm. One false
affordance remains: the "→ Page 2" lines under the findings look like links but aren't.

### UX-17 (S2): The forward lines on P1 look like links

- *What I see:* every finding card ends with "→ Page 2: revenue management and the kiosks." (and three more). Arrow,
  Night text, own line: the web's link pattern. They are static text boxes (`link_line()`, `opzet_report.py:367`).
  The docstring says the Next button does the navigation, on purpose ("one control").
- *Why it matters:* a juror who clicks one gets no response, which feels like a broken link at exactly the moment they
  want to go deeper. It's also a missed chance: the line already names the right place on page 2.
- *Recommendation (pick one):*
  - **(a) Better:** make each line a button with a **bookmark** action that opens page 2 in the right state. F1/F2 go
    to Recommended (the hero rows); F3 and F4 go to "What waits, and why". A bookmark button changes page and state in
    one click and works under Publish to Web. It needs a hover state (underline or Night → blue) and alt text.
  - **(b) Cheaper:** drop the arrow and write "Answered on page 2: …", so the line stops promising a click.
- *Conflict check:* principle P-2 ("one control") in doc 21. Bjorn's call: (a) adds four controls but they are the
  most natural ones a reader will try.

### UX-18 (S2): Page 2 Recommended is the densest view, and it's the one that opens

- *What I see (21, and 31 at 1366 px):*
  - At 1920 px the view holds a ten-row hero table with seven columns, a recommendation tile with four KPIs, two
    sentences and a four-row tracking table, a stacked tranche bar with three label blocks, a paragraph and a chart.
  - At 1366 the page renders ~1223 px wide (scale ~0.64): 16 px body text becomes ~10 px, and the tracking table
    ("Finding / What we track / 2025 / 2032 with A / Status", two-line cells, codes like "0 of 20 · 0") ~9 px.
  - The 1366 capture (31) is readable on a sharp screen but not comfortable.
- *Why it matters:* a juror on a laptop meets the report's decision page as small print. It also pulls the page away
  from "beautiful": V2's opening view had one dominant chart, V3's has two tables.
- *Recommendation:* remove one block from this view.
  - The best candidate is the **tracking table** in the recommendation tile. Move it to a tooltip on the tile ("Hover
    for what we track") or into "What waits, and why". Keep the four KPIs, the two sentences and the CTA.
  - The tile then gets air, and the rest can step up to 16 px at its rendered size.
  - Second candidate: the tranche-label block above the bar (three lines per tranche) can lose its third line (the
    "a year once live" facts are in the hero table).
- Check one capture at 1366 again afterwards.

### UX-19 (S2): F1's "Commissions" label names the wrong thing

- *What I see (20):* the grey upper line ends at ~45% with "Commissions" right after it; the blue lower line ends with
  "After commissions". The shaded area between them is what commissions take, but the label sits on the line end, so
  a first reader takes the upper line to be "Commissions" (a series at 45%?).
- *Recommendation:* name the upper line "Before commissions". Put "Commissions" in small grey inside the shaded band
  (or "Commissions and costs", if the note's 1.0 + 1.8 split is the point). This is the first chart on page 1, so it
  deserves zero doubt.

### UX-20 (S2): "Hover a year" sits on the wrong element on P2 Recommended

- *What I see (21):* the tag is at the top right of the "$60M plan" card, next to "Not needed · $8.7M", directly above
  the tranche bar. The chart that opens the year tooltip starts ~140 px lower, at the bottom right. Doc 18's rule ("the
  cue sits on the thing you point at") is broken here: the tag reads as "hover the tranche bar". In What waits and
  Build the same tag is right, because there the chart fills the card.
- *Recommendation:* move the Recommended tag to the chart's own top row, right-aligned at the chart's right edge
  (beside "The 2028 gate", clear of the "High +$19.2M" label), or put it under the x axis next to the milestone line.

### UX-21 (S3): The F4 strip plot needs a two-second key

- *What I see (20):*
  - Three rows (Airport 82.0%, City 79.1%, Highway 77.5%) with a diamond at each type's average. The diamonds have no
    key. The averages are printed next to the row names, so a careful reader works it out; a scanning one doesn't.
  - Hotel names sit left of their dot (Phoenix ●, Kuala Lumpur ●), right of it (● Toronto), or under it (Orlando).
  - "Gatwick, Montréal" labels two dots.
  - The dots jitter vertically, so the three rows blur together between 75% and 85%.
- *Recommendation:*
  - Add "◆ = type average" to the row labels' header (or one small line under the axis).
  - Put all names on one side (right of the dot), except where they hit the edge.
  - Give the three rows a faint row line or alternate tint so the jitter stays inside its row.

### UX-22 (S3): KPI codes out of order

The strip reads "F4 · Occupancy / F1 · Operating margin / F2 · Room revenue… / F3 · Guest quality". The order follows
the headline ("Full rooms, a slipping margin, … middleman, … welcome"), which is right, but the codes then count
4-1-2-3 above cards that count 1-2-3-4. *Recommendation:* drop the codes from the KPI labels (the colours and words
already map them to the cards), or renumber the findings in headline order. Renumbering touches every F-reference on
both pages, so dropping is the cheap option.

### UX-23 (S3): Negative numbers have two colours

- In **Build** (09, 10) negative NPV values and bars are orange, and positive bars are Night.
- In **Recommended** (21) the same values ("−$0.6M", "−$27.3M") are Night/grey text, and the bars take their colour
  from the move's status (blue A, Night 2028 gate, grey not now).
- So the kiosks are a blue bar on one tab and an orange bar on the next.
- *Recommendation:* in Build, colour the bars by plan membership (Night) and keep orange only for the over-budget
  alarm and the rule line; negative values in the default text colour, as in the hero table. Or the other way round,
  but one rule per page.

### UX-24 (S3): The plan chart's missing negative tick (rest of UX-07)

On P2 Recommended the plan chart ticks only +$20M and $0M (FinalV3 README: "two axis ticks instead of four"). Low ends
at −$4.6M below the zero line with no scale under it. The label carries the number, so this is only a sense-of-scale
issue. Add a −$10M tick, or set the axis to −$10M to +$20M in steps of $10M.

### UX-25 (S3): Tooltip seams

- **tt-package (29):** the page is 616 px high for an eight-move package, so for A (three moves) ~150 px of white
  sits under the table. If Power BI can't fit the height, move the table above the sentence block so the gap ends up
  at the bottom of a lighter block, or cap the page at about six moves and show "+2 more".
- **tt-f4-hotel (27):** "Occupancy by month 2025" has a ~30 px plot with three ticks (100% / 80% / 60%) stacked on
  top of each other. Give it 60-70 px, or keep only the 90% reference and the two line-end values.
- **tt-year (30):** "−$24.7M  none yet": C's value and "Your plan / none yet" touch. One more column gap, or "—" for
  your plan.

### UX-26 (S3): "What waits, and why" is a wall of prose

Five columns of 16 px sentences, 4-5 lines per cell (24). The decision words exist ("Not planned.", "At the 2028
gate", "Back at the 2028 gate") but they're buried mid-cell. *Recommendation:* put each cell's verdict first in
semibold ("Not planned." / "2028 gate." / "2028 gate, with a beyond-2032 case."), then the reason, in "Why not now" and
"When we decide". Replace "no NPV shown" with what is meant (e.g. "NPV: see the hero table" or "not cash positive by
2032"); as it stands it reads like a missing value.

### UX-27 (S3): A and B overlap

In every view A's disc covers part of B's (24, 25, 09). They are 0.3 apart in NPV and $2.25M apart in capex, so at this
scale they collide. Offset B's disc label (letter outside the disc, with a leader) or draw both discs at 80% size.

### UX-28 (S3): The P2 subtitle carries the method

"The other $50.6M waits for one Board decision in mid-2028. Expected case. NPV through 2032 at 8% in 2025 dollars is the
contest's rule and counts nothing after 2032, so we also show what each move earns a year once it is live." That's two
lines under the strongest headline in the report. Keep the first sentence (and the case word, which the scenario
pills change). The rule and the "so we also show" reasoning belong in the source line (it already says "8% discount
rate, 2025 dollars, horizon 2032") and the glossary ("A year by 2032").

### UX-29 (S3): Build plan tile seams

- "NPV through 2032" wraps to two lines while "capex", "a year by 2032" and "a year on cost" don't (09, 10), so the
  four KPIs lose their common baseline. "NPV, 2032" or a slightly narrower number column fixes it.
- Between the rules line and the bars there's a ~50 px gap that changes with the number of moves; top-align the bars
  to it.
- In the selector header "NPV" and "A year by 2032" nearly touch; right-align both and give the last column 8 px more.

### UX-30 (watch): Power BI's "Click here to follow" bubble

After a tab click the service shows its own tooltip bubble while the mouse rests on the button (24, 25). At 1366 px it
hangs over the scenario pills until the mouse moves. Accepted in doc 23 (Q11); nothing to do. Mention it only if a
juror note asks about "overlapping tooltips".
