## 1.3 Analytical Quality · 8.5

**Where it hits.**
- The search is complete and stated: "None of the 456 packages beats A through 2032" with "Only 6 pay back by 2032,
  all under $15M" (09). In the Low case the title turns honest instead of hiding: "In the Low case no move pays back
  by 2032; package A still loses least of all 456 packages" (texts/06).
- Two explicit tests sit in the hero table's header: "8%: our cost of money" and "pays back by 2032" (05). The title
  names the one move in A that fails a test: "the kiosks earn 20.6% a year on cost but pay back after 2032".
- NPV and cumulative cash are reconciled where they meet: "Package A, cumulative cash, $M (not discounted, so it ends
  above NPV)" (texts/05).
- Caveats are where a judge needs them: "an association, not proof of cause" (01), "The data cannot price the cost
  of waiting, so this case is understated" (09), "A rule, not a forecast" (05), "2021 is a depressed base year" (01).
- Sums I checked hold: the plan adds to $60.0M (texts/05); six moves in Build add to +$6.1M a year
  (2.4 + 0.8 + 0.4 + 0.8 + 1.0 + 0.7, texts/17); the tracker's "half the fall recovered" matches F1 (33.7% to 35.1%
  against a 2.8-point fall, texts/01 and texts/05).

**Where it misses.**
- **J3-01 · Major · the refresh tranche against the page's own tests.** Capture 05. The plan reserves $29.5M,
  half the budget, for "Tranche 3 · the refresh" at "7.9% a year on cost" and −$27.3M NPV: it fails both tests the
  hero table sets. The energy programme passes test 1 (11.8%) and loses less (−$12.2M), yet it is "not now" because
  "Energy and Boston exceed the $8.7M left". The $8.7M is only what is left after the refresh is earmarked. The reason
  for the refresh is given on another view (09: the hotels renovated in 2021 "held NPS near +6 while the rest fell to
  +2.7"; the cost of waiting cannot be priced), and it is a fair strategic reason. But no view sets the refresh and
  energy side by side. A judge testing "well-supported conclusions" reads the hero table top to bottom and sees the
  plan reserve half the money for its last-ranked move.
- **J3-02 · Minor · A against B on the page's own second lens.** The subtitle says "NPV counts nothing after 2032, so
  we also show what each move earns a year once live" (texts/05). On that lens B earns more: "+$4.0M a year by 2032"
  against A's +$3.5M (texts/09). In the High case B also ends ahead on cash, +$19.7M against +$19.2M, and is $0.1M
  behind on NPV (texts/11). B's "Why not now" cites only NPV and the queues. A still has the better yield (my
  arithmetic: $4.0M / $11.7M is about 34%, against A's 37.5%), but the page never says so.
- **J3-03 · Minor · "The app carries it" changes measure by case.** Expected: "+$3.4M of the +$3.3M" (NPV). High:
  "+$9.5M of the +$12.7M" (NPV). Low: "+$0.6M of the +$1.0M" (a year by 2032, since A's Low NPV is −$5.5M)
  (texts/05, 06, 07). Same sentence, different measure, not said. "$3.4M of the $3.3M" (a part larger than the
  whole) also makes a reader stop.
- **J3-04 · Minor · Build's parts do not add up.** With A picked, the "A year by 2032" column reads +$2.4M, +$0.8M
  and +$0.4M (texts/16), which is $3.6M. The plan says +$3.5M. The footer on the same view says "Parts are rounded to
