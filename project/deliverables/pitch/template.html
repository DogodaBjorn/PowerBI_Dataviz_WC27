One brand, one budget, three ways to make the Board look up.

Goodnight Inn is Pillow & Key Group's budget brand: 50 hotels, 6,485 rooms, a $60 M investment budget against $100.7 M of proposed projects. Below are three report directions that differ in kind, each in its own look, each drawn with the real numbers from the contest dataset. Every chart here is a hypothesis to re-derive in the model before it ships.

Direction 1 · editorial briefing · unit of analysis: the brand

Wake-up call

A one-page briefing the chair reads before the meeting, and a second page that is the decision itself. Headline, three findings, a plan. The report is the argument; nothing on it is furniture.

seven-pass score50/70highest floor

Analytical spine

Two pieces of evidence most entries will not have: the channel counterfactual (what 2025 would have cost at the 2021 channel mix) and the 2021 renovation cohort as a control group for the satisfaction decline.

Signature moment

The folio. A hotel bill that shows an OTA guest and a direct guest pay the same $109 and leave $90 vs $107 on the desk.

Look & feel

Paper white, navy ink, one lamp-amber accent. A display serif for hero numbers, a grotesk for labels, a mono for kickers. Three story columns under a one-line headline, the way a briefing is written.

Risk

Words. A briefing lives on copy; weak copy turns it into a text document with charts, and the KPI band is one lazy build away from a card wall.

Not funded, and why
  • Boston Logan new hotel — $24.75 M is 41 % of the budget, opens March 2029, $2.0 M a year at full run-rate: a 12-year payback.
  • Kingston extension — the hotel runs 61 % and never passed 74 % in two years; the Low scenario loses money.
  • Energy programme — $15 M for $1.8 M a year; energy already fell from 5.9 % to 4.9 % of revenue without capex. Phase 2, after the horizon.
  • Breakfast upgrade — F&B is 3.8 % of revenue; a 30 % lift is $0.8 M a year. Fund from the reserve if the Board wants it.
Risks & trade-offs
  • Rooms vs kilowatts. The refresh and the energy programme do not both fit beside the digital moves and the extensions; we chose the one that answers the satisfaction decline.
  • Disruption. 15 % of 1,180 rooms out of service for 14 months is $6.6 M of lost revenue inside the horizon, counted in full with no cost relief.
  • App adoption is the swing. The app's Low and High cases are $0.6 M and $4.1 M a year; everything else moves within ±$0.4 M.
  • Reserve. $8.7 M unallocated is about 30 % of the refresh capex, held as contingency.

Direction 2 · operations wall · unit of analysis: the hotel

Fifty keys

Behind every reception desk is a wall of key hooks. Goodnight Inn is fifty hotels, and every investment decision is made hotel by hotel: this one is full, this one is tired, this one we leave alone. Page 1 is the wall tonight; page 2 is the wall as the plan lights it up, year by year.

seven-pass score49/70highest ceiling

Analytical spine

A hotel-level segmentation nobody reaches from brand totals: seven hotels are full, twelve are tired (eight of them in the refresh list), one is weak. Plus capacity timing from the organisers' own demand assumptions: Toronto runs out of rooms in 2028, Montréal 2029, Gatwick 2030.

Signature moment

The wall lighting up as the year slider runs from 2027 to 2032. Native: one matrix, conditional formatting driven by a year slicer.

Look & feel

Night navy, amber key-card lights, white condensed type. The wall is allowed to be dense because it is one visual; everything around it is a margin note.

Risk

Fifty rows at 1080 px is ~16 px per row. Every chip must print its value, every amber must pass AA on navy, and the Board may read it as an operations screen. Matrix render time under Publish-to-Web is tested on day one.

Direction 3 · finance ledger · unit of analysis: the year

Two futures

The Board is shown 2032 twice. First the brand if nothing is funded, projected with the organisers' own assumptions: the market fills the rooms to 90 %, every one of those dollars arrives at a thinner margin, and eight hotels run out of rooms. Then 2032 with the plan, as a band, not a line.

seven-pass score48/70strongest spine

Analytical spine

It uses the table most entries will ignore (FinanceAssumptions), decomposes the baseline into volume, price, cost and channel, and states uncertainty with the Low / Expected / High scenarios the organisers provided.

Signature moment

The fan: one line splitting into a grey do-nothing future and a green funded band.

Look & feel

Ledger paper with faint green rules, ink, one accent, typewriter figures. Two large charts and a column of numbers per page, like an annual-report spread.

Risk

Honesty optics. The baseline is scenario logic; any projected mark without its label invites a "simulated data" objection. Finance-first, so the hotel-level "why" is thin.

the dataset codex · PillowKey_Dataset.xlsx · 16 tables · 17 single-direction relationships · no measures

Everything that is in the data, and what each piece is good for

Verified: revenue and opex lines sum exactly to their totals; channel room revenue and nights reconcile exactly to MonthlyPerformance; no nulls in the facts; 161–165 hotels per month (late openers). Figures below are Goodnight Inn unless marked group.

Traps to write into the metric contracts

  1. 2021 is a COVID-shaped base year; trend claims use 2022/2023 → 2025.
  2. Guest scores are averages: weight every aggregate by SurveyResponses.
  3. NPS is already a net score (−100…100), not a 0–10 score; never average it with the others or format it as a percentage.
  4. Late openers (Valencia 2023, Porto 2022) distort growth; use like-for-like sets.
  5. Six hotels have no renovation date; product age = years since opening.
  6. Whether DistributionCost sits inside OtherOperatingCost is unstated; pick one assumption, never double-count.
  7. Impacts are in 2025 dollars; a FinanceAssumptions baseline is nominal. Never mix on one axis without saying so.
  8. Portfolio Low / High = sum of each project's Low / High; never mix scenarios across projects.
  9. Scenario is a disconnected table; new-property projects have no hotel rows; DependsOn / ExclusiveWith are text.
  10. Hidden tooltip pages are allowed; any other hidden page risks the two-page rule.

Not in the data (do not fabricate)

  • Daily rates, lead times, cancellations, length of stay below channel averages
  • Market or competitor data (no RevPAR index), market share
  • App or loyalty members, marketing spend, booking sources below channel
  • Staff headcount, energy in kWh, review text
  • Financing, tax, depreciation, cash, a terminal value beyond 2032
  • Any demand figure other than the FinanceAssumptions growth rates

The organisers' rules, read from FinanceParameters

  • Discount rate 8 % to 2026 · base year 2025, impacts in 2025 dollars
  • Ramp-up 50 % in months 1–12 after completion, 85 % in 13–24, 100 % from 25
  • Capex spent evenly over the project duration
  • Rooms out of service earn nothing during works
  • No start before January 2027; impact measured through December 2032
