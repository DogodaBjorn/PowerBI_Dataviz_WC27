## 1 · The headline

**From 6.0 to 8.3 out of 10. Doc 19 returned the strategy. This version I would approve, with two conditions in the
minutes.**

This is not the same report with fixes on top. The case has a new spine:

- **The headline now sells the return:** "Fix the middleman first: $9.4M now earns $3.5M a year by 2032." In Low it
  reads "$1.0M a year". The headline is honest in every case.
- **There are two lenses, side by side, in the opening view.** The hero table puts *a year on cost* next to *NPV
  through 2032*, with an 8 % cost-of-money line. The subtitle owns the horizon in one sentence: "the contest's rule
  … counts nothing after 2032, so we also show what each move earns a year once it is live." This was my main
  objection in doc 19, and it is answered completely.
- **The $50.6M has become a capital plan:** three tranches, one Board meeting in mid-2028, a written decision rule,
  and $8.7M not needed.
- **There is a 2032 scorecard:** distribution cost 11.3 % → 10.1 %, margin after commissions 33.7 % → 35.1 %, 20 of
  20 kiosk hotels, rooms added. Each line has a plain-language status ("half the rise since 2021 undone").
- **Every P1 finding points forward** ("→ Page 2: …"), and every P2 row points back (F1–F4).

To a committee this now reads like a board paper: what we fund, what it returns, what can go wrong, what waits, and
when we decide again.

## 3 · What the new version raises

A better case gets a harder read. Once the plan has a gate, the committee reads the gate's terms closely.

| ID | Sev. | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| INV-CA-6 | **Major** | **Tranche 2 is tied to the app, but the extensions don't depend on it.** The gate releases the airport rooms "if distribution cost has fallen at least 0.6 points … *and* Gatwick and Toronto still topped 90 %". The extensions' economics depend on occupancy, not on the app. And your own Low view shows that the extensions are the *resilient* moves. In Low, Gatwick earns 13.3 % a year on cost and the app 12.1 %. Tranche 2 still clears the 8 % cost of money in Low (11.7 %). The gate makes the most robust move wait on the most volatile one. There may be a good reason, for example "new rooms sold through agencies at $19.68 a night earn less, so we fix the channel first". If so, it has to be on the page. Otherwise a committee member will propose splitting the condition |
| INV-RK-5 | **Major** | **The gate's threshold reads weaker than it is.** "Fallen at least 0.6 points (the app's Low case)" reads like a floor. The app goes live in Oct 2027 and earns 50 % of its effect in its first 12 months (the finance rules). So at a mid-2028 meeting, a 0.6-point fall is where the **Expected** case stands (half of 1.2). The Low case would show about 0.3. The rule is stricter than its label, and the Board can't tell. Say over which months it is measured and what it means ("on track for the Expected case") |
| INV-RK-6 | **Major** | **The biggest cheque has the softest rule.** Tranche 2 ($12.4M) has two measurable conditions. ## 5 · Experience: convincing and enjoyable?

**P1 — 9.0. A pleasure, and now a better argument.** The forward links ("→ Page 2: the booking app with member
rates") do something that doc 19 asked P2 to do: the Board leaves page 1 knowing what page 2 will propose. The F4
strip of 50 hotels with Toronto, Gatwick and Montréal past the 90 % line, and Kingston alone at 61 %, says "full"
better than the old bars. F1's split of the 2.8 points is the kind of honesty that makes a committee trust the
rest.

**P2 — 7.5. It convinces. It still asks for concentration.**
- The opening view now answers the Board's four questions in reading order: what each move earns (left), what we
  fund and what it returns (right), what waits and when we decide (bottom). That is the right structure.
- **"Think you can beat A? Build your own →"** is the best line in the report. It invites the sceptic in, and in
  Build the sceptic now sees yield as well as NPV.
- **What waits, and why** is a model alternatives table. Option, numbers, why it's on the table, why not now, when we
  decide: a board secretary could minute from it.
- The cost is density. At laptop size the hero table and scorecard are small, and the Recommended view needs a few
  minutes of reading. For a pre-read that is acceptable. As a live screen in a meeting it is heavy.

**How convincing:** the **problem** 9.5 / 10 (was 9). **Tranche 1** 9 / 10 (was 7). **The five-year strategy**
7.5 / 10 (was 4). This version does what doc 19 said was missing: it answers "where should the $60M go?", not only
"what do we fund first?".

## 6 · Verdict: would I invest?

**In you: yes, and more firmly than on 13 Nov.** In doc 19 I backed the analyst and asked for the strategist. This
version shows the strategist: the capital plan, the gate, the scorecard and the honest "deferred" are decisions,
not analysis. And it shows the thing investors back most: you took hard feedback, rebuilt the case around it, and
kept the voice and the rigour. You didn't patch the old one. That is coachability under deadline. It is rarer than
talent.

**In package A: yes, fund Tranche 1 now.** It is unchanged and better argued: 37.5 % a year on cost, the downside
bounded at −$5.5M with +$1.0M a year still coming in, and the dependency on the app stated openly.

**In the strategy: yes, approved, with two conditions for the minutes.**
1. **Tranche 2:** explain why the airport rooms wait for the app, or release them on occupancy alone (INV-CA-6),
   and state the 0.6-point rule as "on track for Expected, measured over …" (INV-RK-5).
2. **Tranche 3:** give the $29.5M a criterion the Board can watch, with NPS in the rule (INV-RK-6).

**Committee decision:** *Strategy approved. Tranche 1 funded. The 2028 gate adopted, subject to conditions 1 and 2.
Management to name an owner for staff cost outside this plan (INV-AC-3).*
