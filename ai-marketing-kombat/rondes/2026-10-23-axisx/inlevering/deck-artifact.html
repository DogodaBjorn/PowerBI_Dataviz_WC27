The Founding 500 · how we get there

Not ads. Not avatars. The founder asks, and AI does her research.

01Communities

Women 39+ already talk about perimenopause in forums, groups and newsletters. AI reads those conversations at theme level: what women ask, which words they use, and what each community allows.

02The founder asks

She writes to the people who lead those communities, in her own words, asking for help: "10 places held for your group." We pick them by their public role in the group. Every message is written and sent by hand.

03Founders Circle

500 women who recognise what is happening and want to build the app they wish existed. Free in beta, a founding price at launch, three invites each.

The brief: 500 women onboarded, 60% open the app on day 7, $5,000 without paid media; a separate $50,000 scale test. What the brief does not ask for: big numbers, women who do not yet recognise perimenopause, paying users now, or a plan B that is simply plan A times ten. Details: appendix §1 and §2.
Why communities: women learn about menopause from family (61%) and friends (59%) at least as often as from doctors (51%): Gallup-Pivotal 2026. Why no influencers: 15% of women trust influencers for health information, against 86% for healthcare providers: KFF 2026.

What the Founding 500 experience · the engine

She tells her story. AI listens for intent. People decide.

01AI labels her words

AI reads her answers for six things: does she recognise perimenopause, what has she already tried, does the method suit her, how committed is she, how urgent is it, what is her life situation. It judges what she says, never how well she writes. A person reviews every unclear case.

02Two groups

Signal group (about 85%): women whose answers show those signals. Contrast group (about 15%): women of the same age without them, picked at random. The contrast group shows what happens when we select less strictly.

03One ID, four events

Her email address ties the quiz, the invite and the app login together. We count four moments: onboarding done, app opened, feedback given, doctor page created. "Opened the app on day 7" means the same thing for every woman and every source.

Trust rules: separate consent for health data, her raw answers deleted after analysis, no health-related data to any ad platform, no woman rejected by a machine alone, a weekly check that the AI treats every group the same. Appendix §4.
GDPR art. 9 and 22: gdpr-info.eu · Perimenopause needs no test to be recognised, so no woman is excluded for lacking a diagnosis: NICE NG23 · Apple's TestFlight sends beta invites by email, so each tester stays linked to her quiz: developer.apple.com.

What the funnel teaches us for plan B

Every visit is tagged. Five numbers come out.

Visitors per woman

How many visitors it takes to onboard one woman, per step and per source. We expect about 10 to 1. In plan B this number turns the price of a click into the price of a woman.

Signal vs contrast

Day-7 opens, feedback and day-14 pages, for both groups. The gap shows how much drops off when we select less strictly. That is what paid traffic will look like.

Intent profile

Which answers and earlier actions predict a day-7 open. From then on we can judge the quality of any channel on day 0, before retention data exists.

Founding price yes

The share of women who say yes to the founding price. No payment yet. The first measure of willingness to pay.

Winning video

The opening video (hook) that brings in women who still open the app on day 7, not the one with the most clicks. It becomes the first paid ad.

The score the brief asks for: 500 onboarded, 60% open the app on day 7. The numbers we steer on along the way: feedback in week 1, the day-14 page, the founding-price yes.

Measurement plan, definitions and stop rules: appendix §5. All targets are assumptions until measured. Each community gets its own invite code, so every number can be read per source. 500 onboarded and 300 active on day 7 are goals, not results.

Building plan B · the $50,000 scale test

Scale only what costs less than a subscription earns back.

Break-even: what a subscription earns back
Stage 1 · $12k

Try four channels: Meta UK and US, paid newsletters and creators, Google Search, Reddit. Learn what one onboarded woman costs in each.

Stage 2 · $15k

Triple the daily budget on the best two channels. Watch two things: what a day-7 active woman costs, and what share of applicants show the signals.

Stage 3 · $18k

Push the best channel to ten times plan A's weekly pace. Find the point where cost starts to climb, and why: the audience runs out, the video wears out, or people see it too often.

$5k stays in reserve for a holdout test (a group that sees no ads, to prove the ads work) and for new videos. Next stage only if the cost per day-7 active woman and the signal share stay within 25% of stage 1. Up 50% or more: we stop.

Starting assumption: Meta charges about $20.70 per 1,000 views for health and wellness ads (Triple Whale via Influee). Plan A's real numbers replace it. Stages, channels and rules: appendix §6.
6/8
Building plan B · the ceiling and the honest line

What a subscription earns back decides how far paid can go.

The ceiling

The limit is not how many women exist. $50k comes nowhere near exhausting the US; in the Nordics it does. The real limit is the slice of women who recognise perimenopause and want help, and how fast the videos wear out.

Early warning: if a channel's share of signal-group applicants drops, it is running out of the right women. We see that within days; day-7 data takes a week.

The honest sum

One paying member brings in £85 a year after Apple's cut. On public benchmarks, 2.9% to 6.2% of downloads become payers. At about $23 per onboarded woman, paid ads do not earn themselves back in year 1.

The $50k does not buy growth. It buys two answers: do women selected on intent become payers two to four times as often as the benchmark, and at what spend does the cost per woman start to climb?

Conversion benchmarks: RevenueCat 2026 · Populations and iOS share: ONS, US Census, Eurostat, StatCounter · Health ad restrictions: Meta, Google · Apple keeps 15%: Small Business Program · Worked sums: appendix §6 and §7.

The money

Ten dollars a woman to learn. Twenty-three to find out if it scales.

$5,000
Plan A · $3,700 to build before the first invite, $1,300 to run

Build: funnel and analytics $1,200 · AI community research $500 · videos and member cards with real women $1,500 · community space $500. Run: AI previews and classification $400 · partner reports $200 · reserve $700. AI stack: Claude for research, previews and classification; Runway for captions, cuts and card rendering; no AI-generated people. $10 a woman is the average, not the price: an extra woman costs cents in tokens. The founder's hours (about 40) are not in the $5,000.

$50,000
Plan B · about $23 per onboarded woman, ~1,900 women, 3 stages

$12k to try four channels, $15k to triple the best two, $18k to push the best one to ten times plan A's pace, $5k for the holdout test and new videos. Starts only once plan A has delivered its five numbers (slide 4).

The number that links plan A to plan B is not a cost. It is the share of women who say yes to the founding price. That share decides whether $23 a woman can ever earn itself back.
