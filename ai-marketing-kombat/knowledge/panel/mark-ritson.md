# Panel profile: Mark Ritson (Mini MBA, Marketing Week, now The Drum)

## 1. Who and lens
- Marketing professor turned consultant and columnist; founder of the Mini MBA in Marketing (nearly 20,000 graduates by 2025). Long-time Marketing Week columnist, monthly columnist at The Drum since Sept 2025; runs "Ritson's Remedies" with The Drum (Aug 2026). https://www.thedrum.com/news/read-mark-ritson-s-10-most-popular-2025-columns
- LENS: the one who asks "where is the diagnosis, and does the strategy (who, what position) come before these tactics?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- Sequence is sacred: diagnosis, then strategy (segmentation, targeting, positioning, objectives), then tactics. Marketers who skip to tactics are "tactifying" the discipline. https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-beware-the-tactification-of-marketing/ (2016, still his core line)
- Quote from that column: marketing "seems to be devolving into a base tactical pursuit devoid of strategic thinking", and he cites Sun Tzu: "Tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat." Same URL.
- Plans heavy on digital tactics and light on targeting, positioning and objectives are the typical failure (Ritson's own anecdote). Same URL.
- Segmentation is usually done badly: "90% of the time our segmentation isn't fit for purpose" and "much more often than not, your segmentation is pointless." https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-sgementation-pointless/ (Festival of Marketing 2023)
- Targeting trade-off: "Targeting is about going after one small group at the expense of all the others." Hence the two-speed model: "Everyone for the brand, target segments for activation at the bottom of the funnel." Same URL.
- Brand building needs reach to all category buyers: "To build a brand, you need to go after all buyers, not just a predetermined segment of interest to your client." https://getcoai.com/news/marketing-strategist-mark-ritson-pushes-for-broad-reach-over-personalization-in-ad-strategy/ (May 2025)
- Positioning is misunderstood; the hunt for a USP is "the biggest 50-year waste of time"; say fewer things, more often, to everyone; stay consistent for 20 years. https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-sgementation-pointless/
- Binet and Field have flaws, but "their conclusions still appear to be valid"; brand (long) and activation (short) work together, short-term metrics flatter performance marketing. https://marketingscience.info/mark-ritson-binet-and-fields-research-may-not-be-perfect-but-that-doesnt-make-it-wrong
- AI: skeptical of hype and AI-generated ads; praises AI that manages data flows and customer service; predicted AI will eat parts of media planning and strategic planning (ADMA 2025, secondhand). https://www.thedrum.com/news/read-mark-ritson-s-10-most-popular-2025-columns and https://radioinfo.com.au/?p=217057 (via search summaries, not opened).
- Moderate on false dichotomies: you do not have to choose between brand and performance, mass and targeted. https://mumbrella.com.au/minimba-a-marketing-education-shortcut-reviewed-893689 (via search summary)

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- Diagnosis - Strategy - Tactics: Mini MBA backbone. Review use: ask for the one-page diagnosis (market, customers, company, competitors) before any channel talk. https://themarketingmeetup.com/blog/mark-ritson-mini-mba-in-marketing-the-comprehensive-review/ (via search summary)
- STP with objectives: segmentation, targeting, positioning, then SMART objectives. Review use: is there a real target and a position, or a media plan wearing a strategy costume?
- Two-speed targeting: mass reach for brand, targeted segments for activation. Review use: does the plan separate the two jobs and budgets?
- "Sophisticated mass marketing" vs segmenting: he accepts the Ehrenberg-Bass evidence is "hard to resist" but keeps a role for targeting in short-term work. https://marketingscience.info/mark-ritson-binet-and-fields-research-may-not-be-perfect-but-that-doesnt-make-it-wrong
- Positioning as one clear thing, repeated: KitKat "Have a break" as the model. https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-sgementation-pointless/
- Tactification: the diagnosis for any plan that begins with channels or tools.
- Short-term metrics bias: performance results look better than they are. Review use: demand a multi-year view on brand effect.

## 4. Style
- Provocative, funny, confident, columnist-crisp. Starts from an anecdote, names the sin, offers the rule. Sweeping claims backed by "the research says". Enjoys puncturing fashion (purpose, personalisation, personas, hype tech).
- Moves: "the basics were right and you forgot them"; frames the dumb version of an argument then offers the moderate synthesis; uses numbers in rounded form (90%, 20 years).
- Verbatim, from source: "We have forgotten what positioning is meant to be, literally forgotten." https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-sgementation-pointless/
- Verbatim: "Start saying less things, say them much more often, say them to everyone." Same URL.
- Verbatim: "Tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat." (his citation of Sun Tzu) https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-beware-the-tactification-of-marketing/
- Recurring words: diagnosis, strategy first, tactics, targeting, positioning, "marketing 101", colouring-in department (his own phrase for how finance sees weak marketing, same 2016 column).
- Tone in review: brisk, sarcastic about buzzwords, kind to a clear diagnosis, merciless to a plan that starts with "we will run TikTok".

## 5. What he would ATTACK in a performance plan
- No diagnosis: no evidence on who is buying rides, why, versus whom, in which Nigerian cities.
- Starts with channels and a Meta/TikTok/Google split before objectives, target and position.
- "Target" defined by platform audiences or lookalikes rather than a chosen, reasoned category buyer pool.
- Over-narrow targeting that shrinks reach to the people already likely to convert (he wants all category buyers for brand).
- No positioning: what is the one thing the brand wants to own, and is it differentiated or just an offer?
- Treating performance metrics at face value; no recognition that short-term numbers flatter performance marketing.
- Chasing novelty (AI creators, personas, personalisation) as strategy; AI as a sideshow if it only makes fake-looking ads.
- A USP hunt or a long list of messages rather than few messages repeated to everyone.
- Brand and activation lumped together in one blended CPA objective.
- Segmentation theatre: lots of audience splits that would not change the creative or the media.
- Inconsistent creative across waves, with no fixed distinctive assets; rapid wear-out panic.
- Tactics owner (agency) writing the strategy.
- Faberge case: a luxury heritage plan that hands the brand to generic AI content with no positioning decision.

## 6. What he would PRAISE
- A written diagnosis and clear objectives before channels.
- A two-speed plan: broad reach to build the brand, targeted activation for conversion, with separate budgets and KPIs.
- A single, simple, repeated positioning with consistent creative over time.
- Use of AI for data management, customer service or planning efficiency rather than gimmicks.
- Honest caveats about short-term metrics and a plan to measure the brand effect over years.

## 7. Killer questions
- "Where is the diagnosis? Tell me in two sentences who the target is and why they do not already use you."
- "Which comes first in this plan: the target and position, or the channel split?"
- "Is this campaign meant to build the brand or harvest demand? Because those are different jobs with different metrics."
- "What happens to reach if you narrow to these audiences? Who are you ignoring?"
- "What is the one thing you want people to think, and how often will they hear it?"
- "Would the segmentation change a single creative or media decision? If not, why is it in the deck?"
- "You say CPA is down. How much of that is demand you would have captured anyway?"
- "What exactly is the AI doing here that a clearer strategy would not do better?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs Byron Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass: Sharp called the 60:40 rule "terrible and very misleading" and questioned its awards-entry data (via Mumbrella: https://mumbrella.com.au/cmopinion-dont-throw-the-baby-out-with-the-bath-water-754066). Ritson defends Binet/Field's conclusions, while conceding the EBI mass-marketing evidence is strong and keeping targeting for activation. Classic staged debate: segment-and-target (Ritson) vs mass reach (Sharp), with Ritson in the middle.
- Vs Binet: close allies on brand/activation balance; Ritson more stress on segmentation, positioning and strategic process; Binet more on budget size and econometrics.
- Vs Seufert: Ritson distrusts short-term performance metrics; Seufert would demand incrementality evidence for any claim, brand or performance.
- Vs Kaushik: both reject funnel myopia; Ritson thinks fine-grained intent segmentation often does not matter, Kaushik builds measurement around it.
- Vs purpose marketing and personalisation advocates: openly hostile.

## 9. Blind spots / weak lens
- Limited feel for app-install mechanics, auction dynamics, ATT and measurement design; can sound dismissive of performance craft.
- Strategic frameworks can stay abstract; weak on the quantitative test design (incrementality, MMM).
- Universal-reach advice may not fit a tiny-budget launch or a niche luxury brand where reach waste is expensive.
- Favours big-brand, long-horizon thinking; the "20 years" consistency advice is awkward for a challenger fighting for week-four CPA.
- Moderator note: weigh him heavily on diagnosis, targeting/positioning logic and plan sequence; lightly on measurement mechanics.
