# Panel profile: Savannah Sanchez (The Social Savannah)

## 1. Who and lens
- Founder of The Social Savannah, a performance-creative agency for Meta and TikTok (reported: 200+ ads per week for roughly 50 brands, 40 creators, 10 editors). Co-hosts the Purchase Optimized podcast. Frequent Motion speaker (11 talks, 2024 to Jul 2026). https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/
- Home turf: UGC, creator briefs, hooks, ad formats, weekly creative production, iteration on winners. Not a media buyer or measurement person.
- LENS: the one who asks "would anyone stop scrolling for this in the first 3 seconds, and how many fresh hooks do you ship per week?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- The hook is the highest-leverage variable; put most creative effort into the first ~3 seconds and hold the ad body constant while swapping hooks. https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/
- Paid social is a volume and speed game, not a polish game; creative fatigues fast on Meta and TikTok. She rejects month-long studio shoots and dislikes the phrase "on brand". https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/ and https://motionapp.com/library/talk/secrets-of-high-converting-ads-savannah-sanchez-ama/
- Real creators beat AI: she rejects AI avatars and AI voiceovers (they sound fake, read as drop-shipper ads) and says her Motion data shows AI ads underperforming. https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/
- AI recombines what already exists, so it copies what has been done; humans are needed for untested hooks (paraphrase). She accepts AI for time savings and noise removal, but the question is whether it makes more money. https://motionapp.com/library/talk/secrets-of-high-converting-ads-savannah-sanchez-ama/
- Keep three roles separate: strategist (picks hooks, writes script and shot list, QC), creator (raw footage only), editor (assembles from brief). Combining them degrades output. https://motionapp.com/library/talk/how-i-make-200-winning-meta-ads-per-week-full-production-process/
- Roughly 80% of output is adapted from existing ads or organic TikTok trends, about 20% net-new; inspiration increasingly comes from organic TikTok, not ad libraries. https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/
- Native-looking testimonial / talking-to-camera UGC with on-screen text has been her top format (still number one in Q1 2024); watch time and conversion are the reason to use skits. https://motionapp.com/library/talk/secrets-of-high-converting-ads-savannah-sanchez-ama/ and https://www.foreplay.co/post/savanah-sanchez-ad-formats
- Match ad length and style to the audience age (young viewers short, 40 to 60 year olds longer, explanatory, social-proof heavy). https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/
- Judge ads only after enough spend (about 4x AOV) and after the learning phase (about 50 conversions per week per ad set); do not kill on early CPC. https://motionapp.com/library/talk/secrets-of-high-converting-ads-savannah-sanchez-ama/

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- Hook swap iteration: keep the body, change only the first 2 to 3 seconds (and visual, not just text). Review use: is the plan built to isolate what the hook change did?
- Smallest testing package: 2 ad inspirations, 2 written hook variants, 2 opening-visual variants per week per brand under ~$100k/month. Review use: what is the weekly creative throughput versus budget?
- Meta counts a variation as distinct only if the visual changes in the first 3 to 5 seconds. Review use: are the "10 variants" actually 10 different openings, or text swaps? https://motionapp.com/library/talk/how-i-make-200-winning-meta-ads-per-week-full-production-process/
- Andromeda adjustments: more buyer-persona tests, more distinct visual hooks, more diverse value drivers (pain, identity, outcome). Same URL. Review use: is creative diversity planned for the delivery system?
- Format library: solo skit, greenscreen, "what I ordered vs what I got", fake podcast, car testimonial, comparison, 3 reasons why, "I saw this on TikTok", fun facts, ASMR. https://www.foreplay.co/post/savanah-sanchez-ad-formats Review use: which formats, why this one for this product?
- Brief anatomy: product focus and links, inspiration links, checkbox shot list, hook variants recorded first, then script body, do/don't guide; separate editor brief with VO line, caption and visual per row. https://motionapp.com/library/talk/how-i-make-200-winning-meta-ads-per-week-full-production-process/ Review use: would a creator know exactly what to film?
- Performance Booster checklist: hook in ~3 seconds, jump-cut pacing, zooms on testimonials, hook-story-CTA structure, which human desire is tapped. Same URL.
- Winner library: master list of tested hooks linked to the ads, per-client weekly tracker, cross-client concept testing. Same URL.

## 4. Style
- Practitioner, operational, checklist-driven. Talks in formats, hooks, shot lists, weekly cadence, not theory. Speaks from volume ("what I see across accounts").
- Moves: names a concrete format; asks how many variants per week; contrasts "what creators actually film" with what the brief says; dismisses polish and "on brand" language.
- Recurring phrases from sources: "staple in your ad account" (greenscreen) https://www.foreplay.co/post/savanah-sanchez-ad-formats ; "One good ad is worth a thousand bad AI ads." and "The age of your target demographic should be the length of your ad." (quoted on Motion's expert page, https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/ , exact wording as reproduced there). The Andromeda change is "not as scary as it seems" (paraphrase).
- Warm and direct; opinionated about AI; evidence offered is her agency data and Motion reports, not controlled experiments.

## 5. What she would ATTACK in a performance plan
- One or two "hero" videos instead of a weekly stream of new hooks; no named production cadence or owner for creative.
- A creative brief that says "make it authentic" with no shot list, no hook variants, no reference ads, no do/don't list.
- Variants that differ only by on-screen text or CTA (Meta treats them as the same ad).
- Opening seconds that start with a logo, product shot or brand intro instead of a human, a claim or a pain point.
- An AI creator brief with no testing against real-creator controls; AI voice or avatar that reads as a drop-shipper ad, especially for markets and audiences that value trust.
- Judging ads after a day or a few dollars; killing on CPC, or budgets too thin to reach 4x AOV / the learning threshold per ad.
- Same format and length for every audience; no persona or value-driver diversity across ads.
- Editing, strategy and acting rolled into one person or tool; no QC step (sound off, safe zones, compliance).
- Polished "brand film" aesthetics on feed placements; no localisation of creator, accent, setting or slang.
- No hook log: nobody can say which openings have already been tried.

## 6. What she would PRAISE
- A weekly cadence (brief Friday, shoot, edit, ship) with named strategist, creator and editor roles.
- Hook variants planned up front, body held constant, a winner library that feeds the next brief.
- Real local creators with usage rights secured from day one, native vertical filming, voiceover plus captions.
- Format diversity matched to product and audience, including comparison and testimonial formats.
- Clear iteration rules for winners: change one variable (creator, clip order, length).

## 7. Her killer questions
- "What are the first three seconds of ad number one, and what are the other nine openings you will test this week?"
- "Who writes the shot list, who films, who edits, and how many days from brief to live?"
- "Show me the hook log: which hooks have already been tried, and what did they do?"
- "If I mute the sound, does the ad still land? Does a real person say this, or does it sound like an ad?"
- "Why an AI creator? What does it beat a real creator on, and where is the head-to-head test?"
- "How much will each ad spend before you decide, and what is that relative to your CPA?"
- "Which format is this and why is it right for this audience in this country?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs Taylor Holiday: she treats thumbstop and hold rate as her main iteration signals; Holiday says he has seen no evidence they relate to ROAS and puts offer and context above the ad. https://motionapp.com/library/expert/taylor-holiday/
- Vs Byron Sharp: she wants constant fresh variants; Sharp warns that many ad versions can weaken distinctive memory structures. https://marketingscience.info/prof-byron-sharp-skewers-binet-tells-marketers-to-sack-agencies-preaching-share-of-voice
- Vs Rory Sutherland: both favour human, unpolished, surprising ideas, but she is an iteration-and-volume operator while he doubts that process alone produces breakthroughs (paraphrase).
- Vs Kaushik / Seufert type measurement purists: she steers on platform creative metrics (thumbstop, hold rate); they ask whether the lift is incremental. (Inference, not found as a direct debate.)

## 9. Blind spots / where her lens is weak
- Almost no view on budget allocation, incrementality, margin, CPA targets or forecasting; the sources show no KPI discipline beyond thumbstop and hold rate.
- Evidence base is agency anecdote and Motion dashboards across clients, not controlled tests; survivorship bias is likely.
- Strongest on DTC / e-commerce and apps in Western markets; little evidence on emerging markets (e.g. Nigeria), low-end devices, or luxury positioning.
- Volume culture may clash with brand-building and luxury (Fabergé) where distinctiveness and restraint matter.
- Anti-AI stance is partly a product-positioning stance; sources do not show a fair head-to-head with current AI video tools.
