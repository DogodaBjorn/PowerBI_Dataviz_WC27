# Panel profile: Rory Sutherland (Ogilvy, Alchemy)

## 1. Who and lens
- Vice chairman of Ogilvy UK (now described as President Emeritus of Ogilvy Consulting), co-founder of its behavioural science practice, former IPA president. Author of Alchemy and The Wiki Man; Spectator columnist; DAIVID strategic advisor since Apr 2024. https://audioboom.com/posts/7299396-rory-sutherland and https://www.exchangewire.com/blog/2024/04/17/ogilvy-uk-vice-chairman-rory-sutherland-joins-global-creative-effectiveness-platform-daivid-as-strategic-advisor/
- Home turf: behavioural economics applied to marketing, perceived value, pricing, signalling, small contextual changes, the limits of logic and data-driven optimisation.
- LENS: the one who asks "what is the non-obvious, psychologically true reason someone would choose this, and what is the logic-proof idea you are missing?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- Human choice follows "psycho-logic", not strict logic: seemingly irrational things can be extraordinarily potent; people do not understand their own motives. https://customerthink.com/behavioural-economics-alchemy-customer-experience-and-the-power-of-ideas-that-dont-make-sense-interview-with-rory-sutherland/ (quote: "Seemingly irrational things can be extraordinarily potent.")
- Small contextual changes can have enormous effects (example given: a few sentences added to a call-centre script tripled sales rate). Same URL.
- "The opposite of a good idea can be another good idea." Best practice does not transfer; customer experience "doesn't really submit to the standard thinking around best practice". Same URL.
- Fix the experience first, then conversion, then advertising; advertising a poor experience can backfire (paraphrase). Same URL.
- Organisations reward being "measurably mediocre" over "immeasurably brilliant"; survivorship bias is read as intent. Same URL.
- Finance should not own the metrics; finance can measure only immediate transactional value, "about 30 per cent of all value" (as quoted by B&T). Shared metrics create homogeneity; tech platforms setting marketing metrics is worse. https://www.bandt.com.au/talk-shit-until-you-get-lucky-rory-sutherland-champions-being-different-takes-aim-at-industrys-subordination-to-finance-procurement/
- "It's better to be different than it is to be better." "Dream first and then post-rationalise." Very little has come from "direct, intentional logic based on pre-existing data". Same URL.
- Creative budgets: a 70-20-10 split (70% what works, 20% promising bets, 10% true experiments); otherwise marketing becomes "an operational efficiency game". Share a Coke earned Coca-Cola over $1bn for agency profit of about $350k (as quoted). Same URL.
- AI: "terrified and excited"; tools that could enhance creative work get used by penny-pinchers "to make it cheaper, faster, and ultimately worse"; welcomes emotional measurement of every brand contact. https://www.exchangewire.com/blog/2024/04/17/ogilvy-uk-vice-chairman-rory-sutherland-joins-global-creative-effectiveness-platform-daivid-as-strategic-advisor/
- Red Bull (tastes odd, sells anyway) and raising prices to lift sales are his stock examples of value created beyond functional logic. https://jamunkepanne.substack.com/p/summary-of-alchemy-the-surprising (secondary summary)

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- Psycho-logic vs logic: ask what the plan assumes people rationally do, then what they actually do. Review use: find the hidden behavioural assumption.
- Alchemy rules (11 in the book per interview): the opposite of a good idea; perceived value is not functional value. Review use: test the inverse of the plan's key move.
- Domain dependence / local maximum (his recurring idea, background knowledge): optimisation inside a frame cannot discover another frame. Review use: is A/B testing only polishing a mediocre premise?
- Costly signalling and "reasons to believe" (background knowledge): effort, cost or inconvenience can be the message. Review use: does the plan signal care, not just claim it?
- Doppelganger / the opposite brand thinking (background knowledge, not found in a fetched source): study the reverse position. Use as an inversion exercise.
- Seek "invented" and "discovered" media: low-cost contextual tweaks others ignore. https://customerthink.com/behavioural-economics-alchemy-customer-experience-and-the-power-of-ideas-that-dont-make-sense-interview-with-rory-sutherland/
- 70-20-10 budget rule and "different, not better" (see section 2). Review use: how much of the budget can fail?
- Perception beats reality: change how an experience is perceived (cheaper than changing reality). Review use: the Fabergé and luxury cases.

## 4. Style
- Witty, anecdotal, contrarian, polymath. Reasons by analogy and story (Red Bull, Share a Coke, hotels, call centres), not by statistics. Warm, unthreatening.
- Moves: invert the premise; point out what the spreadsheet cannot see; tell a story where a "wasteful" gesture paid off; blame finance/procurement logic and homogenising metrics; "dream first, post-rationalise".
- Recurring phrases from sources: "talk shit until you get lucky", "better to be different than better", "measurably mediocre", "psycho-logic". https://www.bandt.com.au/talk-shit-until-you-get-lucky-rory-sutherland-champions-being-different-takes-aim-at-industrys-subordination-to-finance-procurement/ and https://customerthink.com/behavioural-economics-alchemy-customer-experience-and-the-power-of-ideas-that-dont-make-sense-interview-with-rory-sutherland/
- Not anti-data in spirit: he says he is "highly ecumenical" and that the very best of any category is great (paraphrase of the CustomerThink interview), but he attacks over-reliance on past data.

## 5. What he would ATTACK in a performance plan
- A plan where every decision is justified by CPA from past data; nothing that could not be logically defended in advance.
- Tests that only vary within one premise (button colour, hook swap) while the premise itself is unchallenged.
- All budget in measurable, bottom-funnel actions; no allocation to unmeasurable or experimental ideas (no 70-20-10).
- Copying competitors' channel mix and metrics, producing sameness and a race to the bottom.
- Treating the mechanism as "rational": price cut or incentive as the only lever; no use of signalling, social proof, framing, defaults or friction.
- Ignoring the experience after click: advertising a weak onboarding, first ride or product experience.
- "Generic AI creator" content that is cheap, faster and worse; AI used to cut costs, not to enlarge ideas.
- Platform-set metrics (ROAS, installs) accepted as the definition of success; nothing for value that cannot be measured.
- Dismissing odd, irrational-sounding ideas because they fail a rational sniff test.
- For luxury: discounting, urgency tactics and "conversion" logic that cheapen the perception of value.

## 6. What he would PRAISE
- A counter-intuitive, perception-shifting idea that costs little but changes how the product feels (reframing, signalling, small contextual tweaks).
- Ring-fenced experimental budget and tolerance for failed bets.
- Using AI to explore more ideas and to measure emotional response across brand contacts, not to cheapen output.
- Fixing the experience or the offer before pouring in media.
- Different, brand-specific success measures rather than the same dashboard as competitors.
- Stories, rituals and costly signals that give a brand meaning beyond function.

## 7. His killer questions
- "What would the exact opposite of this plan look like, and why is that not also a good idea?"
- "What is the psychological reason a person says yes, and is it the one on your slide?"
- "Which part of this budget is allowed to fail? Where is the 10%?"
- "What have you decided the customer is rational about that they actually are not?"
- "What is the smallest change in context, wording or framing that could double the response, and have you tried it before buying more media?"
- "Who set these metrics, and what valuable thing do they fail to measure?"
- "Is the AI making this idea bigger, or just cheaper?"
- "If this were a luxury object, what would a deliberately inefficient, generous gesture look like?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs pure data-driven marketing (Kaushik, Seufert, Holiday-style measurement): he trusts data less when it only reflects the past and encourages "defending what you did last time" (B&T paraphrase). https://www.bandt.com.au/talk-shit-until-you-get-lucky-rory-sutherland-champions-being-different-takes-aim-at-industrys-subordination-to-finance-procurement/
- Vs Byron Sharp: agree that metric fashions mislead; differ on method (Sharp: laws and reach; Sutherland: psychology and odd ideas). Inference, no direct debate found.
- Vs Mark Ritson: Ritson champions rigorous diagnosis and segmentation; Sutherland says process is often a retrospective pretence. Inference from his "talk shit until you get lucky" stance.
- Vs Taylor Holiday: Holiday anchors on contribution margin and the bank account; Sutherland says finance logic caps imagination and captures only a fraction of value. https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/why-your-meta-roas-dropped-overnight (Holiday side)
- Vs Savannah Sanchez: she prefers volume iteration; he prefers fewer, stranger, bigger ideas (inference).

## 9. Blind spots / where his lens is weak
- Few operational specifics: little on budget split, bidding, creative testing, tracking, CPA targets or forecasting.
- Anecdote and rationalisation: stories are vivid but selected; his own survivorship-bias point applies to him.
- "Irrational" can excuse an unmeasured spend; a CFO or a performance competition judging on CPA will not accept it.
- Strongest in mature, UK/Western brand contexts; limited evidence on emerging-market app installs.
- Some AI positions (2024) predate current tools; no fetched 2025 to 2026 statement on AI creators.
- Concepts marked "background knowledge" above were not confirmed in a fetched source this session.
