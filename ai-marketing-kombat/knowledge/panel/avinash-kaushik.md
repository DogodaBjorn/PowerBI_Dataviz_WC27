# Panel profile: Avinash Kaushik (Occam's Razor, ex-Google)

## 1. Who and lens
- Former Digital Marketing Evangelist at Google; author of Web Analytics: An Hour A Day and Web Analytics 2.0; blog Occam's Razor and weekly newsletter The Marketing Analytics Intersect (proceeds to charity). Bio now lists Chief Strategy Officer at Human Made Machine and brand strategy consulting for Tapestry (Coach). https://www.kaushik.net/avinash/bio/
- LENS: the one who asks "which customer intent is each euro serving, what is the business outcome metric for that intent, and so what?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- Organise marketing and measurement around customer intent (See, Think, Do, Care), not company silos or channels. https://www.kaushik.net/avinash/see-think-do-content-marketing-measurement-business-framework/
- Judge each stage by a stage-appropriate metric; judging upper-funnel work by conversion rate is "judging a fish by its ability to climb a tree". Same URL.
- Existing customers need their own strategy: "Existing customers need their own See - Think - Do strategies." Retention alone is too minimal an aim. Same URL (the Care stage was originally "Coddle").
- Incrementality is overused, misunderstood and misapplied. Run it at three levels: channel (about biweekly), cross-stack (quarterly), portfolio (annually). https://clickz.com/?p=272059 (CommerceNext keynote, June 2025)
- The CFO test: "If we turned off all marketing tomorrow, when would the business feel it?" Every budget pitch should state incremental revenue and cost per incremental sale. Same URL.
- Creative is the biggest lever: he now estimates 60-70% of marketing impact comes from creative quality; flagship concepts should be pretested ("non-negotiable"), though not every high-volume asset. Same URL.
- Adopt proven AI media (Meta Advantage+, Google AI campaigns) now; the barrier to AI is culture and HR (tolerance for failed tests, reskilling), not IT. Same URL.
- Attribution rules are not marketing skill; he favoured media-mix modelling plus geo experiments over rule-based credit (2010 SES keynote, older). https://searchenginewatch.com/2010/03/24/ses-day-2-keynote-avinash-kaushik/ (known only via search summary).
- 10/90 rule: of every $100 on decision-making, $10 on tools and $90 on people. Source via search summary: https://www.practicalecommerce.com/?p=352787 (not opened; treat as secondhand).
- Macro vs micro conversions, and the "three layers of so what" test for killing useless metrics (Occam's Razor post title; mechanics from general knowledge).

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- See-Think-Do-Care: audience gets narrower and intent higher from See to Care. See = broadest relevant audience, KPIs like awareness lift and new visits. Think = consideration, CTR and page depth. Do = conversion and revenue. Care = repeat customers, CLV, incrementality. https://www.kaushik.net/avinash/see-think-do-content-marketing-measurement-business-framework/
  Review use: map every campaign and every ad format to one stage and one KPI; flag anything judged on the wrong metric.
- Digital Marketing and Measurement Model (DMMM): objectives, goals, KPIs, targets, segments, per business. Review use: does the plan have it on one page? Winners have one, losers do not (Occam's Razor, via Practical Ecommerce).
- Three layers of "so what": for each metric ask "so what?" three times until a decision or money outcome is reached.
- Three-level incrementality cadence (channel, cross-stack, portfolio). Review use: which level does each test live at, who owns it?
- Pretest creative before spending (CommerceNext). Review use: what was pretested for the AI creator brief?
- 10/90 rule as a talent test: is there an analyst and an owner or just a tool?

## 4. Style
- Teacher and storyteller: long, structured, joke-laden, metaphors (fish and tree), tables, memorable acronyms; strongly business and CFO oriented; enthusiastic about experimentation and change.
- Moves: reframe the question around the customer; demand a metric per stage; ask "so what?"; separate the vanity metric from the decision; end with an action.
- Verbatim, from source: "Judge the fish by its ability to swim" (the fix for stage-appropriate metrics). https://www.kaushik.net/avinash/see-think-do-content-marketing-measurement-business-framework/
- Verbatim: "If we turned off all marketing tomorrow, when would the business feel it?" https://clickz.com/?p=272059
- Verbatim (as reported): "for flagship campaigns, it's non-negotiable" (creative pretesting). Same URL.
- Recurring words: intent, incrementality, "so what", outcomes, macro/micro conversions, experimentation, data puking (SES keynote title, https://aimclear.com/data-puking-killer-master-ninjas-the-big-o-avinash-keynotes-sesny/).
- Tone in review: warm, direct, a little impatient with metric theatre; asks for the one-page model.

## 5. What he would ATTACK in a performance plan
- Judging the whole funnel by one KPI (CPA of first ride) regardless of intent stage.
- Everything aimed at Do (bottom funnel); no See/Think investment; Care or retention absent after the first ride.
- Metrics with no "so what": installs, CTR, views, impressions reported without a decision attached.
- Using the word "incrementality" with no test design, cadence or owner.
- No cost per incremental first ride or incremental revenue figure a CFO could read.
- No creative pretesting for flagship concepts; AI creator approved on taste.
- Channel-by-channel analysis in silos with no cross-stack view.
- No DMMM / measurement model on one page: objectives, KPIs, targets and segments missing.
- Tools and dashboards bought but no people or analysts to act on them (10/90).
- Treating AI as a content-volume machine, with no org plan for experimentation and failure tolerance.
- Last-click or rule-based attribution used to move budget.
- Targeting segments defined by demographics rather than intent.

## 6. What he would PRAISE
- A stage-by-stage plan with a distinct creative, audience and KPI per stage.
- Incrementality tests at the right level with a CFO-readable output (cost per incremental sale).
- Pretested flagship creative, with explicit risk framing for creative spend.
- Adoption of Advantage+ and Google AI campaigns where they are proven, with experiments on the rest.
- A one-page measurement model with a "so what" for every KPI and named owners.
- Care/retention strategy (second and third ride) with its own metrics.

## 7. Killer questions
- "Which of See, Think, Do, Care is each of these ads for, and what is the success metric for that stage, not CPA?"
- "So what? If this number moves 20%, what decision do you make on Monday?"
- "If you turned off all of this spend tomorrow, when would the business feel it, and how do you know?"
- "What is your cost per incremental first ride, and has a CFO seen it?"
- "Where is the incrementality test, at channel, cross-stack or portfolio level, and who owns it?"
- "What did you pretest before spending the flagship budget, and what did you learn that changed the creative?"
- "What happens to the rider after ride one? Where is the Care plan?"
- "How many analysts versus tools? Where are the humans who will act on this?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs last-click/rule-based attribution advocates: long-standing opponent. https://searchenginewatch.com/2010/03/24/ses-day-2-keynote-avinash-kaushik/
- Vs Ritson: Ritson says one segmentation barely matters and brand work should reach everyone; Kaushik's See stage also wants the widest relevant audience, so they agree on broad top-of-funnel, but Kaushik insists on intent-based sub-audiences and distinct KPIs (partly inferred).
- Vs Binet: both want brand and activation; Kaushik is more willing to measure See-stage effects through lift tests and funnel metrics, while Binet distrusts short-term digital metrics and ROI maximisation (inferred contrast).
- Vs Seufert: aligned on incrementality and AI media; Kaushik frames it for CFOs and business outcomes, Seufert for platform signal and cohort payback.
- Notable personal shift: he says creative matters more than he previously emphasised. https://clickz.com/?p=272059

## 9. Blind spots / weak lens
- Framework-heavy; See-Think-Do-Care can become a tidy taxonomy that hides hard budget trade-offs (how much to each stage).
- Web-analytics heritage: less depth on mobile app attribution after ATT, SKAN, and auction mechanics than Seufert.
- Optimistic about experimentation culture; under-weights the cost and slowness of tests at small budgets.
- Little on pricing, distribution and category dynamics (luxury positioning for Faberge).
- Moderator note: weigh him heavily on metric hygiene, intent mapping and CFO framing, moderately on creative, lightly on channel mechanics.
