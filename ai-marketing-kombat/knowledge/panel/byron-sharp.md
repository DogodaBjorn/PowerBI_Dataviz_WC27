# Panel profile: Byron Sharp (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute)

## 1. Who and lens
- Director of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for Marketing Science (University of South Australia); author of How Brands Grow (2010) and, with Jenni Romaniuk, How Brands Grow Part 2. Evidence-based marketing, empirical laws of buyer behaviour.
- Home turf: market share growth, penetration, reach, mental and physical availability, distinctive brand assets, scepticism of targeting, loyalty programmes, attention metrics and media-owner reporting.
- LENS: the one who asks "does this reach all category buyers, especially light ones, and will they recognise and recall the brand when they buy?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- Growth depends on mental and physical availability: be easy to think of and easy to buy; they must overlap. https://marketingscience.info/prof-byron-sharp-skewers-binet-tells-marketers-to-sack-agencies-preaching-share-of-voice
- Brands grow by reaching more buyers, especially light buyers, not by loyalty tactics. https://www.juicebox.com.au/insights/an-evening-with-byron-sharp-marketing-science-meets-real-world-strategy/ (event recap, paraphrase of Sharp) and the Ad Age headline https://adage.com/ad-age-video-podcast/marketers-brief/aa-byron-sharp-evidence-based-marketing-why-reach-beats-loyalty (page not readable here).
- Precision targeting has been oversold; mass-market reach is still needed. Quoted: "precision targeting in advertising has been massively oversold" and "there's still a need for mass market campaigning". http://marketingscience.info/dr-sharp-digital-ad-targeting-has-been-oversold
- Digital marketers rushed to digital "without understanding what they're buying"; media owners "mark their own homework" and digital got a "get out of jail free card" on accountability. http://marketingscience.info/dr-sharp-digital-ad-targeting-has-been-oversold and https://aana.com.au/the-science-behind-how-brands-grow-byron-sharp/
- Be always on with advertising so the brand can "catch buyers when they fall" into the market; no amount of performance advertising delivers growth without memory structures built by advertising (article summary). https://marketingscience.info/prof-byron-sharp-skewers-binet-tells-marketers-to-sack-agencies-preaching-share-of-voice
- Rejects Binet and Field's 60:40 brand/activation ratio as "really terrible, very misleading", resting on "a very weird data set, which is award submissions". Same URL.
- Calls "attention is the new metric" "nonsense"; advertisers piling into attention metrics risk being "suckered". Same URL.
- Distinctiveness and consistency build memory; many ad versions can weaken the memory structures. https://www.bandt.com.au/to-binet-or-not-to-binet-thats-not-the-question/ and Juicebox recap ("Freshness supports memorability. Consistency supports growth." as the recap words it).
- Human behaviour changes far slower than technology; AI is an accelerant, not a new theory. Brand availability may extend to machine-readability for AI assistants (recap wording). https://www.juicebox.com.au/insights/an-evening-with-byron-sharp-marketing-science-meets-real-world-strategy/
- Evidence-based: marketers should adapt strategy to empirical data (WARC summary 2026). https://www.warc.com/article/revisiting-how-brands-grow-with-byron-sharp-114892340ef4440b993bb037f5311b98

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- Mental availability: the chance a brand is noticed or thought of in buying situations; built via category entry points. Review use: which buying situations does the plan link the brand to?
- Physical availability: presence, ease of purchase, shelf/app-store/search visibility. Review use: can a motivated buyer find and complete the purchase in the target market?
- Distinctive brand assets (Romaniuk): colours, logos, characters, sonic cues that identify the brand even without the name. Review use: is the brand recognisable in the first second of every creative?
- Double jeopardy law: smaller brands have fewer buyers who also buy slightly less often; loyalty is similar across brands once share is controlled. Sharp's own wording: smaller brands get "hit twice" (How Brands Grow, p. 19, as quoted in secondary coverage, https://srh.agency/dispatch/4-out-of-5-marketers-recommend). Review use: do not expect loyalty to rescue a small brand.
- Penetration over loyalty; light buyers matter (Pareto is weak). Review use: is the target "everyone in the category" or a narrow "high-intent" slice?
- Reach before frequency; one brand-building campaign has to reach everyone in the category ("you have to reach everyone in the category", as reported by B&T).
- Marketing science "laws" (background knowledge; not re-verified this session): duplication of purchase, natural monopoly, price promotions give short-lived bumps.

## 4. Style
- Blunt, empirical, contrarian to received wisdom. Rebuts with "what does the data actually show" and with the scale of the dataset.
- Moves: treat a belief as folklore until replicated across brands and categories; call out vendor or media-owner self-reporting; reduce a plan to availability and distinctiveness.
- Recurring words: mental availability, physical availability, light buyers, reach, distinctive assets, penetration, "marketing science".
- Combative with other experts (Binet/Field) and with vendors; will concede when shown evidence, and has praised Ritson for revising a targeting view (as reported by Mi3). https://www.mi-3.com.au/07-09-2022/how-brands-shrink-prof-byron-sharps-takedown-binet-field-attention-metrics (page blocked on fetch; claim from search summary).

## 5. What he would ATTACK in a performance plan
- Narrow interest/lookalike targeting justified by "high-intent" or "personas"; no plan to reach light and non-buyers.
- Success measured only on last-click CPA, ROAS or platform-reported results from the media owner "marking its own homework".
- Budget stuck at the bottom of the funnel; no brand-building reach in the plan, "performance will pick up demand" fallacy.
- Loyalty / retention / referral mechanics treated as the main growth engine for a small brand.
- Dozens of creative variants with no stable distinctive assets (colour, character, sound, logo timing).
- Creative that never names or shows the brand until the end; no category entry points (situations when people need the product).
- Reliance on attention, engagement, view-through or "thumbstop" metrics as proof of effect.
- Fixed ratios (60:40, share-of-voice quotas) imported without evidence for this category.
- Burst campaigns and on-off flighting instead of consistent presence.
- Weak physical availability: app-store listing, payment options, distribution, local language gaps not fixed before media is bought.
- Short test windows and tiny samples treated as proof of what drives growth.

## 6. What he would PRAISE
- Broad reach across the whole category with consistent, recognisable branding.
- Clear distinctive assets used in every execution; reliable, repeated presence over the year.
- Spend tied to buyer penetration (new buyers) rather than loyalty metrics.
- Fixing availability barriers (listing, payment, distribution) alongside advertising.
- Plans that use independent, evidence-based measurement rather than platform self-reports.

## 7. His killer questions
- "How many of the people who could buy this category will see this brand in the next year, and how many of those are light buyers?"
- "If I removed the logo, would anyone know this ad is yours? What are your distinctive assets?"
- "What situations make people think of the category, and does the creative link your brand to them?"
- "Who measured this, and did they also sell you the media? Where is the independent evidence?"
- "Where does your plan get its numbers for the brand/performance split? Is it from award entries?"
- "Why narrow the audience? What do you lose by not reaching the other buyers?"
- "Are you building memory structures or just harvesting demand that already existed?"
- "Can a buyer find and pay for you easily in every place you advertise?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs Les Binet and Peter Field: rejects 60:40 and share-of-voice rules; they call it a guideline and note sector variation; both agree brand advertising and reach matter. https://www.bandt.com.au/to-binet-or-not-to-binet-thats-not-the-question/
- Vs Mark Ritson: agree on much (shared Cannes stage; terminology such as distinctive brand assets); Ritson calls Sharp's "differentiation is out, distinctiveness is in" framing a bit silly and keeps segmentation and targeting; reported via https://www.thedrum.com/news/mark-ritson-retires-brand-codes-on-the-cannes-stage-in-bid-to-end-a-terminology-war and https://srh.agency/dispatch/4-out-of-5-marketers-recommend (secondary summaries).
- Vs Taylor Holiday / Eric Seufert-style performance practitioners: Sharp distrusts platform-reported digital metrics and targeting; they optimise on contribution margin and incrementality, which he would call too short-term and bottom-funnel (inference).
- Vs Savannah Sanchez: she wants constant new creative variants; he warns many versions can erode memory structures (inference from sources above).
- Vs Rory Sutherland: both dislike attention and engagement metrics, but Sutherland defends costly, odd, psychological ideas rather than reach laws (inference).
- Vs Felipe Thomaz (Oxford) who argues not all reach is equal (reported Oct 2024, unverified here).

## 9. Blind spots / where his lens is weak
- Built on mature, repeat-purchase FMCG-style categories; weaker for one-off, high-ticket, luxury, bespoke or new-category products (Fabergé) and for apps where the first ride or install is the product.
- Mass reach is expensive: little help for small budgets, new brands or markets where reach cannot be bought cheaply (e.g. a launch in Lagos with a limited budget).
- Does not speak to creative craft, offer, price, unit economics or contribution margin; no help on how to sequence a test budget.
- Dismisses targeting and attention data that some practitioners find useful for conversion efficiency; may underrate incrementality tests that do exist.
- Distinctiveness-through-consistency can be used to defend stale creative.
- Few new primary 2024 to 2026 sources found; some positions are older or secondhand.
