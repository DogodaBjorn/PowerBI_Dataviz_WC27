# Luxury marketing principles (for a performance marketer)

Status: reference, 2026-10-23. Sources: Kapferer & Bastien "The Luxury Strategy" (general knowledge, no live lookup), industry practice. Items marked [CHECK] need verification before use in front of the jury.

## 1. The anti-laws of luxury marketing (Kapferer & Bastien), working summary

WARNING: the book lists ~24 anti-laws; the table below is a 10-row working paraphrase of the main ideas, written from memory with no live lookup. Numbering and titles are NOT verbatim. Do not quote as "Law N" in front of the jury [CHECK against the book if you want to cite]. Safe to cite the core idea: "luxury runs on different rules from classic marketing: don't follow the customer, don't discount, don't democratise, keep non-buyers dreaming, price signals value".

| Anti-law | What it means | Practical consequence for media/AI work |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Forget about "positioning" | Luxury is not defined by being better than a rival on an attribute; it defines its own universe, not a point on a map | No competitor-comparison ads, no "best/cheapest/most X" claims. Message = the house's own story |
| 2. Dare to be different, not "customer-driven" | Luxury leads taste, does not follow surveys | Use AI for insight and trend detection, not to "give customers what the data says". Creative led by the house's point of view |
| 3. Don't pander to consumers' wishes | Creator-driven, not demand-driven | Do not let bidding/personalisation algorithms steer the product or the tone |
| 4. Keep non-enthusiasts dreaming | Most people who know the brand will never buy; they sustain its prestige | Reach/awareness has value beyond conversion. Do not exclude the "dreamers" from brand-building media |
| 5. Don't be afraid of high prices; price is a signal | Price is part of the product; raising price can raise desirability | Never discount. No promo codes, no "sale", no price-led ads |
| 6. Keep raising the price | Price elasticity is inverted at the top; value comes from the brand | Price must not appear in performance copy as a hook |
| 7. Do not respond to trends / the market | Luxury sets cycles; heritage over fashion | Social listening informs, never dictates |
| 8. Do not communicate to sell; sell to communicate | Each sale is also a brand act (the experience, the packaging, the clienteling) | KPI must include experience quality, not only revenue |
| 9. Do not sell in the usual way: control distribution | Selective distribution protects scarcity and service | Care with marketplaces, grey market, affiliates, wholesale price integrity |
| 10. Luxury is not about retail: it is about the "flow" of desirability | Brand heat must be managed; overexposure kills desire | Frequency caps, controlled retargeting, no mass-market tactics |

## 2. Brand equity vs performance: the core tension

- Performance marketing optimises measurable short-term response; luxury value sits in brand heat, desire and price power which are slow and hard to attribute.
- Rule of thumb: performance supports the brand, never leads it. The funnel is long: desire (years) -> consideration (weeks to months) -> appointment/enquiry -> purchase (often assisted by a human).
- Measurement truth: last-click will badly over-credit brand search and under-credit everything that created the desire. Use brand-lift, share of search, MMM/incrementality (see KPI section).
- Conversion volume is low (a high-jewellery client may buy once a year or once a decade). Models need value-based signals and offline data, not 50 conversions per week.

## 3. Never discount

- No sale, no codes, no limited-time-price urgency, no "from EUR X" hooks in prospecting.
- Alternatives that preserve value: complimentary engraving or personalisation, gift wrapping, atelier visit, early access, private viewing, "reserved for you" language.
- In feeds/Shopping: do not run price-based product groups; exclude markdown items; keep price consistent across regions/currencies to avoid grey-market arbitrage.
- Wholesale/retailer partners: protect price integrity; monitor unauthorised discounting.

## 4. Scarcity and desirability

- Real scarcity only: limited editions, one-of-a-kind pieces, atelier capacity, waiting lists, numbered series, hand-finished production time. Fake scarcity ("only 3 left!") is a brand-damage tactic.
- Language: "by appointment", "commissioned", "reserved", "a unique piece" rather than "buy now".
- Waiting-list and enquiry mechanics are a feature; design funnels that reward patience (private preview invitations).
- Desirability is also created by exclusion: not every ad reaches every person. Targeting breadth for awareness is fine; discounting-style retargeting is not.

## 5. Heritage and craftsmanship storytelling

- Pillars: founder myth, savoir-faire (hand work, enamel, gem-setting, years of apprenticeship), materials and provenance (stones, metals), archives/museum pieces, famous patrons.
- Show process and time: "300 hours", workshop macro shots, the maker's hands, drawings and wax models.
- Heritage must be alive: link archive to contemporary design; avoid museum-only nostalgia.
- Proof beats claim: certificates, hallmarks, named stones, provenance documentation.
- Story is audience-specific: collectors value archive/rarity; gifters value meaning and occasion; younger HNW value design and values (provenance, sustainability).

## 6. Clienteling (the real conversion engine)

- High-value luxury sales are relationship sales: a named advisor, notes on the client (anniversaries, stones, sizes, family), personal outreach (WhatsApp, WeChat, phone, email), invitations to events.
- Digital's job: generate qualified conversations and give advisors context, not to replace the advisor.
- Data rules: explicit consent, GDPR, minimal data, never creepy ("we saw you looked at...").
- Metrics: advisor-client ratio, repeat rate, share of wallet, response time to enquiries, appointment-to-sale rate.

## 7. Omnichannel reality

| Channel | Role | Measurement note |
|---|---|---|
| Boutique / flagship | Experience, service, highest AOV | Needs offline conversion import / store-visit signals; attribution is partial |
| Own e-commerce | Entry-price pieces, gifting, discovery, research | Digital attribution works best here, but under-reports the influence on boutique sales |
| Wholesale / multi-brand jewellers / department stores | Reach in markets without boutiques | Limited data access; control pricing |
| Auctions (Christie's, Sotheby's) | Prestige and price discovery, collector market, news moments | PR/earned-media value and search spikes, not direct sales |
| Private sales / commissions / bespoke | UHNWI core business, highest margin | Relationship-driven; CRM and advisor logs are the "conversion data" |
| Marketplaces / resale | Often undesirable; authentication and brand control issues | Monitor for counterfeits |

- A search-to-boutique journey is typical: the customer researches on Google, books or visits, buys weeks later. Treat appointments and enquiries as conversions with assigned values.

## 8. HNWI / UHNWI audience behaviour

- Time-poor, privacy-sensitive, advisor-led (wealth managers, concierge, family office, PA), refer to peers. Mobile-first and social-active, but ad-blind to anything that looks like a promotion.
- Long consideration cycles: weeks to years; major purchases tied to life events (engagement, anniversary, birth, inheritance, wedding, wealth events).
- Where they are: Instagram, LinkedIn, premium publications (FT, Financial Times "How To Spend It", Tatler, Vogue, Robb Report), private clubs, events (art fairs, TEFAF, Masters, Cannes, Monaco), WeChat in China, Telegram/WhatsApp for private contact, Google when researching.
- Cultures differ: Gulf (gifting, gold, majlis networks, high personalisation), China (gifting, gold, status, KOLs), US (collector and gifting), Europe/UK (heritage, art). Localise values and calendar [CHECK per market].
- Targeting reality: HNW audiences are mostly modelled (income/affluence proxies, interests, job titles, geography/postcodes, travel behaviour, luxury affinity, lookalikes of existing clients). First-party client data is the best seed (privacy-safe hashed upload).
- Sensible assumption: UHNWI will rarely click an ad; they are reached via referral, events and advisors. Ads' job is to keep the house top-of-mind and capture search when they look.

## 9. Gifting moments

- Core calendar: Christmas/Holiday, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, Easter (key for Fabergé: egg heritage), Lunar New Year, Eid / Ramadan (Gulf), Diwali, Father's Day, Black Friday (avoid - discount culture), wedding and engagement season, birthdays.
- Gifters need reassurance: sizing, delivery by date, packaging, returns, engraving, advisor help. A concierge flow (human or AI-assisted) converts better than catalogue browsing.
- Gifting intent is a search signal (e.g. "gift for wife anniversary jewellery"); also a retargeting-with-care window.

## 10. The role of paid social/search in luxury

| Tool | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brand search (Google) | Capture existing demand; protect the brand name | Always on, exact + phrase, defend against competitors and resellers. High intent, low incremental risk. Test incrementality (brand search often partially cannibalises organic) |
| Generic search ("luxury jewellery", "egg pendant") | Capture category demand | Keep tight; exclude cheap/"discount"/"replica"/"fake" via negative keywords |
| Demand Gen / YouTube / Discovery | Visual storytelling, upper funnel | Premium placements only; brand-lift measurement |
| Performance Max | Use with care: placement and asset control is limited | Asset-group-level exclusions, brand exclusions, curated feed, not for prospecting alone [CHECK current controls] |
| Meta / Instagram | Visual storytelling, reach to affluent audiences, retargeting of engaged users | Frequency caps; Advantage+ audiences are a black box, test against manual |
| Pinterest | Gifting inspiration, jewellery discovery | Good for search/wishlist behaviour |
| LinkedIn | B2B/HNW by job title; corporate gifting; private-client | Expensive, small reach, worth testing for UHNW |
| TikTok | Younger aspirational reach; craft/ASMR content | Brand-safety review; native tone |
| Programmatic / publisher deals | Premium publishers (private marketplaces) | Use allow-lists, never open exchange |
| Email/CRM + WhatsApp | Core owned channel | Highest-margin conversations |

- Retargeting: soft, time-boxed (e.g. 7-30 days), max 2-3 impressions per week, exclude purchasers, never price/discount content, content = craft story or advisor invitation.
- Frequency caps and exclusions are non-negotiable: over-exposure destroys desirability.

## 11. Brand safety and placement control

- Use placement exclusions (apps, kids' content, gaming, low-quality sites, news with conflict/crime topics), inventory type = expanded/limited, topic and keyword exclusions.
- Use curated allow-lists on YouTube channels and publishers.
- Third-party verification (IAS, DoubleVerify) for display/video.
- Brand-sensitive for Fabergé: war/Russia-Ukraine news environments, sanctions articles, auctions of looted art, conflict-mineral stories. Pre-define exclusion topics [CHECK with client].
- Manage AI-generated content too (see ai-disclosure-and-risk.md): no off-brand image, no counterfeits, no wrong-heritage artefacts.

## 12. KPIs luxury uses vs avoids

| Use | Why |
|---|---|
| Brand search volume / share of search | Leading indicator of desire and market share; free via Google Trends/Search Console |
| Branded vs non-branded search ratio | Health of demand creation |
| Qualified appointments / enquiries (with quality score) | Real pipeline for the boutique/advisor |
| Boutique visits / store-visit conversions | Offline impact |
| Lead quality (seniority, geography, budget band, advisor-rated) | Prevents optimisation to junk |
| AOV, value per client, basket mix (entry vs high-value) | Revenue quality |
| Client lifetime value, repeat rate, share of wallet | Core economic outcome |
| Brand lift (ad recall, consideration, intent) | Upper-funnel proof |
| Earned media value, press pickups, auction/PR spikes | Cultural relevance |
| Sentiment, brand perception tracking | Brand health |
| Incremental revenue (geo tests, holdouts), MMM contribution | Causal truth |
| New-to-brand client % | Growth beyond existing CRM |

| Avoid optimising for | Why |
|---|---|
| Cheapest CPM/CPC, CTR, "cost per lead" alone | Pulls in cheap, low-intent, low-fit audiences and placements |
| Volume of clicks / traffic | Rewards the wrong people |
| ROAS on last-click only | Over-credits brand search / retargeting, under-credits creation of desire |
| Promo-driven conversion rate | Teaches clients to wait for discounts |
| Engagement count (likes) without audience quality | Vanity |

## 13. DO's and DON'Ts

DO
- Open with the object, the craft, the story; let negative space and silence do the work.
- Treat the brand as a long-term asset; protect tone, typography, colour palette and imagery.
- Use CRM data (hashed, consented) as the seed for lookalikes and value-based bidding.
- Measure incrementality; separate brand vs prospecting.
- Give a human next step: "Book a private appointment", "Speak with an advisor", "Discover the story".
- Cap frequency; whitelist placements; localise respectfully.
- Keep a human in the loop for every AI-generated asset.

DON'T
- Don't discount, use countdown timers, pop-ups, "limited stock" gimmicks.
- Don't use stock imagery or generic "luxury" clichés (marble, gold glitter, champagne).
- Don't use hard-sell CTAs like "BUY NOW".
- Don't let automated tools (PMax, Advantage+) place ads in unsafe/unknown environments without exclusions.
- Don't chase cheap leads or lower the price tier to hit CPA.
- Don't over-retarget or follow the client round the internet.
- Don't show prices in prospecting; reveal them in the advisor conversation or product page.

## 14. Classic mistakes performance marketers make with luxury brands

1. Optimising for ROAS/CPA on last-click, then shifting budget from awareness to retargeting and watching new-client growth stall.
2. Using cheap-lead objectives (Lead Gen forms, Meta lead ads) that fill the CRM with unqualified enquiries.
3. Running broad PMax/Advantage+ without brand exclusions or placement control.
4. Using promotional copy and discounts (Black Friday, "free shipping over X").
5. Ignoring offline conversions: boutique sales never reach the ad platform, so the algorithm optimises for the wrong behaviour.
6. Treating HNW like mass market: generic lookalikes, wide interest targeting, no income/affluence signals.
7. High frequency retargeting and abandoned-cart style emails.
8. Testing "winning" creatives by CTR and ending up with clickbait or hard-sell visuals.
9. Neglecting search defence: competitors, resellers, counterfeit sites bidding on the brand.
10. Skipping localisation: translation without cultural adaptation.
11. Measuring on a 7-day window for a purchase with a 6-month cycle.
12. Reporting to the client in CTR/CPC language; luxury boards ask about desire, quality of clients, and brand health.

## 15. Quick pitch lines (for the jury)

- "In luxury, the algorithm must learn who a good client looks like, not who clicks cheapest."
- "AI should extend the advisor and the craftsmanship story, never replace them or imitate them."
- "We measure the lift in desire and in qualified appointments, not in clicks."
