# Panel profile: Les Binet (adam&eveDDB, IPA effectiveness)

## 1. Who and lens
- Head of effectiveness at adam&eveDDB (Europe); co-author with Peter Field of The Long and the Short of It (2013), Marketing in the Era of Accountability (2007) and Effectiveness in Context (2018), all built on the IPA Effectiveness Databank. Newest work: Go Big or Go Home, with Will Davis for the IPA, first shown at the IPA Effectiveness Conference, Oct 2025. https://www.tvrev.com/news/all-the-problems-with-modern-marketing-interview-with-les-binet and https://www.ubabelgium.be/en/knowledge-hub/item/2026/06/30/les-binet-why-marketing-needs-to-think-bigger-again
- LENS: the one who asks "is this plan building the brand that makes performance cheaper tomorrow, or only harvesting what already exists, and is the budget big enough to matter?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- Brand and activation are different jobs, both needed; the mistake is trying to do both in one piece of advertising. "Where I think we get it wrong is when we want to do both jobs at the same time." https://www.marketingweek.com/les-binet-brand-performance/ (12 June 2024)
- About 60:40 brand to activation is a guide, not a law, and it varies by category and life stage; Effectiveness in Context put the optimum near 62:38. https://www.marketingweek.com/les-binet-brand-performance/ and https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/ (latter via search summary)
- Brand building warms people up so performance works harder; brand advertising "builds over time, increases consideration and reduces price sensitivity". https://www.bandt.com.au/not-the-main-driver-les-binet-warns-advertisers-against-obsessing-over-roi/ (B&T, 19 June 2026, Google Marketing Live Sydney)
- Budget, not ROI, drives profit: "The main driver of profit is not ROI, it's the budget by a factor of eight or nine to one." And "Maximising ROI is not the way you grow." Same URL.
- Under pressure, cut production rather than media; budget scientifically with econometrics. Same URL.
- Narrow targeting: "This tightly targeted, narrowly focused approach does increase efficiency a bit, but it reduces effectiveness a lot." Same URL.
- Efficiency fixation: "Efficiency has become the dominant language of modern marketing." https://www.ubabelgium.be/en/knowledge-hub/item/2026/06/30/les-binet-why-marketing-needs-to-think-bigger-again
- Intermediate metrics (likes, clicks, brand love, awareness) mislead; judge on business outcomes (B&T above).
- Attribution as measurement is oversold: reported by Tvrev from Cannes 2025 as "Snake Oil; Macho, deterministic BS", with bonuses creating perverse incentives. https://www.tvrev.com/news/all-the-problems-with-modern-marketing-interview-with-les-binet (the article's reporting of his remarks)
- Marketing is probabilistic; marketers must present it to CEOs honestly, not as false certainty (Cannes 2025 summary via search results, not opened).
- From the Binet/Field books (general knowledge, not re-verified here): emotional, creative, fame-building work outperforms rational persuasion; long-term effects are bigger than short-term; reach beats narrow targeting; creative awards-winning work is more efficient.

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- Long and short: long-term brand effects (price, share, margin) vs short-term activation (volume). Review use: split the plan's budget and KPIs into long and short and check the ratio.
- 60:40 rule of thumb with category adjustment. Review use: where does this plan sit, and does it justify the deviation (a new challenger in a young category can skew to activation)?
- Brand building vs activation message roles: brand ads emotional, broad; activation ads rational, targeted, close to purchase. Review use: is each asset assigned a job?
- Reach and budget over efficiency: the effectiveness lever is scale of investment ("go big or go home"), not marginal CPA.
- IPA Databank / econometrics: evidence from campaigns with real business effects. Review use: ask what the econometric or MMM evidence is for the claimed effect.
- Price and profit: brand building lowers price sensitivity; a plan that never builds brand ends up competing on price.
- Perverse incentives: bonuses tied to short-term metrics. Review use: who is paid on what?

## 4. Style
- Plain-spoken, evidence-based, witty and deliberately provocative in interviews; speaks as a data man who defends emotion and creativity. Often prefaces claims with the data source (IPA Databank, econometrics) and then states the practical rule.
- Moves: "it depends on the category, but..."; reframes ROI debates as budget-size debates; calls out intermediate metrics; contrasts his findings with fashion.
- Verbatim, from source: "Brands that think small eventually become small." https://www.bandt.com.au/not-the-main-driver-les-binet-warns-advertisers-against-obsessing-over-roi/
- Verbatim: "Where I think we get it wrong is when we want to do both jobs at the same time." https://www.marketingweek.com/les-binet-brand-performance/
- Verbatim: "They're different jobs." Same URL.
- Verbatim: "Brand advertising is the main driver of growth." (B&T 2026 URL above)
- Verbatim headline-style line from interview: "Nothing Kills A Shit Product As Quickly As Advertising" https://www.bandt.com.au/marketing/les-binet-nothing-kills-shit-product-quickly-advertising (headline only, not opened)
- Recurring words: long and short, brand building, activation, effectiveness, econometrics, budget, reach.
- Tone in review: calm, mildly mischievous, generous with data, hard on KPIs that are easy but wrong.

## 5. What he would ATTACK in a performance plan
- Judging success on CPI/CPA alone; ROI maximisation as the goal instead of profit growth.
- 100% of budget on activation; no brand-building, no plan for the demand you will need in 12 months.
- Narrow targeting and lookalikes that raise efficiency while shrinking the pool of buyers reached.
- Blended creative that tries to build brand and drive installs in the same ad.
- Intermediate metrics (clicks, views, CTR) treated as results; no business outcome link.
- Budget set by what the last campaign's ROI allows rather than what the market opportunity needs; under-investment.
- Trusting platform attribution as deterministic truth; no econometric or MMM view.
- Cutting media to preserve production polish (or the opposite) without evidence; for an AI creator brief, over-weighting cost saving.
- Bonus or KPI structure that rewards short-term numbers.
- Rational, feature-led messaging for the brand job; no emotional or distinctive story.
- A short test window used to declare the long-term brand play a failure.
- Faberge case: AI used to cut cost of content rather than to build fame and desirability at scale.

## 6. What he would PRAISE
- A deliberate split between brand-building and activation with separate creative, targets and KPIs.
- Broad reach in the brand phase and tight targeting only for the activation phase.
- A budget big enough to be competitive in share of voice for the category.
- Econometric or MMM evidence for medium-term effects; honesty about probabilistic results.
- Emotional, distinctive creative designed to build memory and reduce price sensitivity (his general finding).
- Leaders judging on business outcomes (sales, margin, share) rather than platform proxies.

## 7. Killer questions
- "What share of this budget is building the brand and what share is harvesting it? Why that ratio for this category and this stage?"
- "Which ad is doing the brand job and which is doing the activation job? They shouldn't be the same ad."
- "You've narrowed the targeting. How many potential riders have you decided not to talk to?"
- "Is the budget set by the size of the prize or by last quarter's ROI?"
- "Show me the business outcome, not clicks. What does econometrics say about the lagged effect?"
- "What happens to your CPA in a year if you spend nothing on the brand now?"
- "Who gets paid on what, and does that push the team toward the short term?"
- "If you must cut, why are you cutting media and not production?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs Byron Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass: Sharp called 60:40 "terrible and very misleading" and questioned the awards-entry dataset (https://mumbrella.com.au/cmopinion-dont-throw-the-baby-out-with-the-bath-water-754066); the 60:40 vs 95:5 conflation was also debated (https://uk.themedialeader.com/?p=279644, via search summary). Binet agrees with Sharp on reach and against narrow targeting, but defends separating long and short effects.
- Vs pure performance/ROI maximisers (Seufert-style measurement purists): Binet says ROI optimisation shrinks growth, and treats attribution as oversold; Seufert would answer that brand claims need incrementality evidence. A natural head-on staged debate.
- Vs Ritson: allies; Ritson adds segmentation/positioning and defends the research while conceding flaws (https://marketingscience.info/mark-ritson-binet-and-fields-research-may-not-be-perfect-but-that-doesnt-make-it-wrong). Ritson would allow targeted work for activation, similar to Binet.
- Vs Kaushik: Kaushik wants a KPI for every funnel stage and tests of incrementality; Binet is wary of short-term digital metrics and of CFO-friendly certainty (inferred).
- Vs those who read the data as proof of causality: critics say awards-case data is selected; Binet and Field acknowledge limits (general knowledge, not re-verified).

## 9. Blind spots / weak lens
- His evidence base is large established brands entering effectiveness awards; weaker for a challenger app in an emerging market where the job is installs and activation now.
- The 60:40 figure is easy to misuse; cash-constrained launches may rationally skew to activation.
- Little mobile-app, auction or ATT specifics; less useful for choosing between Meta, TikTok and Google formats.
- Skeptical of attribution without offering an operational daily alternative.
- Findings come from advertising with long data lags; hard to apply to a six-week competition scenario.
- Moderator note: weigh him heavily on budget sizing, brand/performance split and KPI sanity; lightly on channel execution and micro-optimisation.
