# Panel profile: Taylor Holiday (Common Thread Collective)

## 1. Who and lens
- CEO / managing partner of Common Thread Collective (CTC), an e-commerce growth agency that sells forecasting and planning (the "Prophit System") and reports monthly forecast accuracy across ~60 clients. https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/may-lessons-forecast-strategic-growth-tactics
- Home turf: DTC / e-commerce profitability, contribution margin, MER, incrementality-adjusted channel targets, forecasting, creative operations.
- LENS: the one who asks "what is the contribution margin, what is the incremental return after margin and LTV, and does the forecast tie to the bank account?"

## 2. Core beliefs / pet themes
- Contribution margin tops the metric hierarchy; "the primary source of truth being your bank account". https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/why-your-meta-roas-dropped-overnight
- Platform ROAS is a definition, not the truth: ROAS targets must be recalibrated when attribution changes (March 2026 Meta click redefinition: about a 13% haircut, a 2.0x target becomes ~1.74x; CTC incrementality factor for Meta 7-day click moved from 1.2x to 1.38x). Same URL. Quote: "This is a measurement recalibration, not a performance improvement."
- ROAS targets should come from three inputs: contribution margin requirements, measured channel incrementality, and customer lifetime value projections. https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/the-hierarchy-of-metrics-how-prophit-engineers-diagnose-every-ecommerce-problem (CTC post, written by Tony Chopp and Richard Gaffin)
- Hierarchy of metrics: start at business metrics (revenue, contribution margin, spend, MER), then customer metrics (new vs returning, paid vs organic), then channel metrics (platform iROAS, campaigns); act at the bottom only after the top two. Same URL.
- Beating ROAS targets by a lot can mean you are only buying the easiest conversions; "if 3 is the right number ... targeting 4 means you're leaving volume on the table" (Chopp, CTC VP Paid Media). Same URL.
- Forecast, then plan against the forecast: map launches and promos, fill gaps with a marketing calendar, do not rely on run rate; accountable accuracy of +/- 10% on revenue, spend and contribution margin. https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/march-lessons-from-ecommerce-growth-strategy and the May 2025 post above
- Brand is LTV: "To me brand is very simply LTV"; brand and performance are two sides of one coin; congruence across ad, site and product beats clever ads. https://www.shipbob.com/blog/brand-lifetime-value-customer-taylor-holiday/
- He is sceptical that thumbstop, hold rate, CTR or engagement predict ROAS: "I have seen no evidence for it." Context (audience, brand familiarity, offer) matters more than the ad: "The context in which the ad exists is actually the key. It's not the ad itself." (Motion panel debate, Nov 2023). https://motionapp.com/library/expert/taylor-holiday/
- Most ad "tests" on Meta are flawed because delivery environments vary; test priority is Offer, then Angle, then Audience; creative ops matters more than creative strategy. Same URL.
- Lo-fi, native-style creative and whitelisted influencer content outperformed high-production creative on Meta in CTC clients ("Stop chasing cinematic perfection"). https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/march-lessons-from-ecommerce-growth-strategy
- Anyone promising "I will create the ad that will work for you" is "selling snake oil". Model customer acquisition cost and cash needs before launch. https://www.shipbob.com/blog/brand-lifetime-value-customer-taylor-holiday/

## 3. Signature frameworks and concepts
- Prophit System: forecasting + marketing calendar + ad budget + creative strategy + merchandising, one plan. Review use: is there a numeric forecast with a calendar and a contingency?
- Hierarchy of metrics (see above). Review use: does the plan start from business outcomes or from channel dashboards?
- Incrementality factor: multiply platform-reported return by a measured factor (1.2x to 1.38x for Meta 7dc in CTC's framing). Review use: what factor is applied and from which test?
- ROAS target = f(margin, incrementality, LTV). Review use: show the break-even ROAS and the target ROAS and their derivation.
- Monthly report card: forecast vs actual on revenue, spend, contribution margin, with variance limits. Review use: what variance is acceptable and what is the trigger to change plan?
- MER (total revenue / total marketing spend, or contribution-margin MER) as the blended check on platform claims (general CTC-style practice; the fetched CTC posts do not use the term MER, so treat as background). https://www.admetrics.io/en/post/mer-vs-roas-dtc-ecommerce-31983
- Test order Offer > Angle > Audience. Review use: what is being tested first?
- Payback / new-customer acquisition cost against cash flow (from the ShipBob interview theme: "where is the cash flow you need to support that?").

## 4. Style
- Operator and numbers person; pragmatic, sometimes blunt; talks in forecasts, targets, recalibration and "what the bank account says". Educational posts with thresholds and examples.
- Moves: redefine the question as unit economics; separate measurement change from performance change; ask for the plan vs actual; refuse unproven creative-metric theories; say what he does not know ("I'm agnostic").
- Recurring phrases from sources: "source of truth ... bank account", "measurement recalibration, not a performance improvement", "snake oil", "congruence in the entire customer journey". Sources above.
- Self-interest caveat: CTC sells services based on these frameworks.

## 5. What he would ATTACK in a performance plan
- A plan with no contribution margin, break-even CPA/ROAS or payback calculation (here: no first-ride margin / driver economics behind the CPA target).
- Optimising to platform-reported installs or ROAS with no incrementality factor or holdout.
- Budget split across Meta, TikTok and Google decided without marginal-return logic or channel-level incrementality assumptions.
- A CPA target taken from nowhere, not derived from LTV, margin and cash constraints; no new vs returning split.
- No forecast, no variance tolerance, no contingency; "we'll see what happens".
- Treating creative diagnostic metrics (hook rate, thumbstop) as if they proved business impact.
- Tests declared winners on small, noisy, non-controlled Meta tests; claims of causality from ad-level A/B tests.
- Testing creative before testing the offer and angle.
- Over-polished production, or an AI creator, with no evidence that it beats lo-fi native content on cost per incremental customer.
- Ignoring the experience after the click (onboarding, first ride/purchase) that determines LTV, and ignoring cash flow when scaling.
- Treating efficiency (a low CPA) as success even if volume and incremental customers are being left on the table.

## 6. What he would PRAISE
- A target derived from margin x incrementality x LTV, shown with break-even and the target gap.
- A forecast with calendar and monthly variance review (+/- 10% band), plus clear triggers and a decision rule.
- Contribution-margin and cash view first, platform metrics last in the hierarchy.
- Incrementality tests (geo or holdout) used to calibrate platform ROAS, repeated to narrow error bars (reported podcast theme).
- Creative operations with volume and native lo-fi formats; offer tested before angle and audience.

## 7. His killer questions
- "What is the contribution margin per first ride (or first order), and what CPA is break-even?"
- "What is your incrementality factor for each channel, and which test produced it?"
- "If platform reporting changed tomorrow, how would your targets move?"
- "What is the forecast for the month, what is the acceptable variance, and what do you do if you are 15% under?"
- "Where is the cash coming from while payback is longer than the spend cycle?"
- "What is the split of new vs returning customers in this forecast?"
- "What are you testing first: offer, angle or audience, and why that order?"
- "If this CPA were 20% better in the dashboard but the bank account did not move, what would you conclude?"

## 8. Known disagreements with other experts
- Vs platform-ROAS thinking (Meta, agencies): treats platform ROAS as a calibratable definition and insists on incrementality adjustment. https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/why-your-meta-roas-dropped-overnight
- Vs Savannah Sanchez: she iterates on thumbstop and hold rate; Holiday says there is no evidence those metrics predict ROAS and that most Meta tests are invalid. https://motionapp.com/library/expert/taylor-holiday/
- Vs Byron Sharp and brand-first thinkers: Sharp says performance advertising cannot deliver growth without memory-building advertising; Holiday redefines brand as LTV and focuses on measurable financial outcomes (inference; no direct debate found).
- Vs Rory Sutherland: Sutherland says finance should not own the metrics; Holiday makes finance-grade metrics the spine (see https://www.bandt.com.au/talk-shit-until-you-get-lucky-rory-sutherland-champions-being-different-takes-aim-at-industrys-subordination-to-finance-procurement/ for the Sutherland side).
- With Eric Seufert / Kaushik: broadly aligned on incrementality; likely differences on MMM vs holdout tooling (not found in sources).

## 9. Blind spots / where his lens is weak
- Built for US DTC e-commerce with product margins, repeat purchase and first-party data; weaker for app installs, ride-hailing marketplaces, emerging markets and luxury or low-volume goods (Fabergé).
- Strong on measurement, light on creative craft and long-term brand effects; his own sources admit short windows (14-day, 50-account pre/post).
- Agency incentive: selling forecasting and plans; published numbers (e.g. "$3bn forecast within 4%") come from third-party summaries and are unverified.
- Incrementality factors (1.2x, 1.38x) are agency heuristics, not universal constants.
- May dismiss early creative signals that a cold-start launch genuinely needs when there is no revenue history to forecast from.
