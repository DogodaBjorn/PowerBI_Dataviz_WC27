How we read the brief

Two clients, two proofs. Find the right 500 women, and measure them so the $50k plan rests on evidence.

What the brief asks

  • Founder: 500 women who complete onboarding, 60% open the app on day 7 (300).
  • Variety of life situations, women who will give feedback, no wave of negativity before launch.
  • $5,000 for tools, tokens and content. No paid media.
  • Investor: a separate $50,000 test of whether acquisition scales, with a ceiling and the numbers from the first 500.

What we decided it is not asking

  • Not volume: 500 random installs fail the founder's goal.
  • Not women in denial: out of scope for the first 500.
  • Not paid conversion now, but a signal of it: the investor needs one.
  • Not plan A times ten: founder time and personal invites do not scale and are not counted in plan B.
  • Day-7 "opened" is the score. We add two steering metrics: feedback given in week 1, and the day-14 doctor report reached.
Source: AxisX brief, Stage 2 (PDF + full brief).

Why we beat AI influencers and synthetic personas

In this category trust is the scarce resource. A made-up woman spends it; a real one earns it.

15%

of women 18-49 trust social media and influencers for health information, vs 86% for health providers (KFF 2026).

61% / 59%

of US women learn about menopause from family and friends; 51% from doctors (Gallup-Pivotal 2026). Peers matter.

5,247

active Meta ads on "perimenopause" in the UK: supplements and pages fronted by named "women" and "doctors" selling bracelets and creams (Meta Ad Library, 23 Oct 2026).

The synthetic-persona format already belongs to the least trusted advertisers. 45% do not trust AI influencers; about 90% want AI content disclosed (Clutch 2026). EU AI Act art. 50 transparency applies from Aug 2026.

What lasts in the category is real: phone-shot women talking to camera with a full sentence on screen ran 200-435 days (Flo, BioID). The founder's interviews say women want to hear from women like them.

What AI does, and what it never does

  • Reads public conversations at theme level: questions, language, community rules. Raw text deleted after analysis.
  • Drafts each outreach note in the founder's voice; she edits and sends it herself.
  • Classifies applicants' own words (next slide) and builds their system preview.
  • Renders system cards, captions and 8 video openings.
  • Never: profiles individuals from health posts, sends automated DMs, generates women or voices.

Loyalty and referral

  • Every applicant locks in a founding price at launch (proposal £70 vs £100).
  • Founding members: free during beta; top 50 feedback givers keep the core plan free.
  • "You shaped this": every change that came from feedback is reported back.

The funnel: she tells her story, AI listens for intent

Landing, open questions, application, invite. Same funnel after the App Store launch.

Four open questions, like the app itself

  1. Tell it like you'd tell a friend: what's changed lately?
  2. What have you already tried to make sense of it?
  3. What would make the next two weeks worth it for you?
  4. What does any plan have to fit around in your life?

Plus age, iPhone, 2 minutes a day for 14 days, email, separate health-data consent, "lock in my founding price".

Hooks (real women, founder first)

"Your doctor has ten minutes." · "Sleep, mood, memory, cycle. All different in the same month." · "You've said 'I'm fine' for months." · "You track your runs, your sleep and your money."

AI labels, people decide

Signals: recognition · actions already taken · fit with the method (own record, doctor prep) · commitment in her words · urgency · life context (quota). Scored on content, not writing style.

The funnel maths

100 visitors → 50 start → 30 finish → 21 apply → 15 invited → 10 onboarded. Steps 1-2 are benchmarks (Adapty: 40-60%, 50-70%); steps 3-5 are our assumptions, measured in week 1.

~715 invites → 500 onboarded → 300 open on day 7. About 3,000-4,000 visitors, warmer sources convert better.

Hard preconditions

  • Email login + 4 events in the app (onboarding_complete, app_open, feedback_given, report_pdf_created), one user ID from quiz to day 7.
  • TestFlight invites by email, not public link (public-link testers are anonymous).
  • Explicit health-data consent; processor agreements.

Where the economics break

A payer is worth £85 net (£100 minus 15% Apple). At $23 per onboarding, year-1 break-even needs ~21% of onboarded women to pay (30% at the £70 founding price). Benchmark: 2.9% median, 6.2% top quartile.

Where the ceiling is

  • Nordic iOS pools saturate inside $50k at frequency 3; the US does not.
  • The real limit is the in-market slice and creative fatigue. Early warning: the share of signal-group applicants per channel drops, days before day-7 data.
  • Health category limits: no health interests on Meta, health events may be blocked, no remarketing on Google. We measure onboarding ourselves.

Stop and scale rules (ours)

Next stage only if cost per day-7 active and signal-group share hold within 25%. Stop a channel if cost per day-7 active rises 50% stage on stage or signal share falls to contrast-group level.

Into the investor deck

Cost curve per channel over three spend levels, signal share per channel, contrast vs signal retention, founding-price and paid conversion, and the break-even line. Scalable means one channel stays under that line.

Definitions from the brief: completed onboarding = finished in full; retained day 7 = opened the app on day 7. Day 0 = onboarding_complete in her local time.
