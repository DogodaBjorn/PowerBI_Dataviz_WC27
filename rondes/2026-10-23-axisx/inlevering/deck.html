AxisX · The Founding 500

What they'll see. What they don't see.

What they see

"She's got everything under control."

A normal workday. The calendar kept. The school run done. The run logged, the sleep tracked, the money sorted. "I'm fine", said again.

What they don't see

"Another night without sleep."

The effort it takes to get through the day. Sleep, mood, memory, cycle, all different in the same month. Notes in five places that never add up. Ten minutes with a doctor and no way to explain what changed.

Not every change is visible. Understanding it should be easier.

The same two lines run through this deck: what the woman shows and what she lives; what the jury sees of the campaign and the engine underneath it.

How we read the brief

Two clients, two proofs. Find the right 500 women, and measure them so the $50k plan rests on evidence.

What the brief asks

  • Founder: 500 women who complete onboarding, 60% open the app on day 7 (300).
  • Variety of life situations, women who will give feedback, no wave of negativity before launch.
  • $5,000 for tools, tokens and content. No paid media.
  • Investor: a separate $50,000 test of whether acquisition scales, with a ceiling and the numbers from the first 500.

What we decided it is not asking

  • Not volume: 500 random installs fail the founder's goal.
  • Not women in denial: out of scope for the first 500.
  • Not paid conversion now, but a signal of it: the investor needs one.
  • Not plan A times ten: founder time and personal invites do not scale and are not counted in plan B.
  • Day-7 "opened" is the score. We add two steering metrics: feedback given in week 1, and the day-14 doctor report reached.

What the brief doesn't see: who she is when nobody is looking. That is where the first 500 are found.

What you don't see · the engine behind plan A

She tells her story. AI listens for intent. People decide.

Underneath each phase

  • Pre-activation: AI reads public conversations at theme level (questions, language, rules), drafts each note in the founder's voice. Leads chosen on public role, never on health posts. Raw text deleted after analysis. She sends every message herself.
  • Landing: one invite code per community, two headline tests, every visit tagged (community, hook, first frame).
  • Open questions: a fixed rubric labels her words: recognition, actions taken, fit with the method, commitment, urgency, life context. Content, not writing style.
  • Selection: signal group (~85%), contrast group without the signals (~15%, random), "later" list. No automated rejection: a person reviews every edge case.
  • One ID: her email links quiz, TestFlight invite and app login. Four events: onboarding_complete, app_open, feedback_given, report_pdf_created.

Trust rules

  • Explicit, separate consent for health data (GDPR art. 9); a person behind every decision (art. 22).
  • Nothing with health meaning ever goes to an ad platform.
  • Applicants not admitted: raw text deleted after 30 days.
  • Weekly bias check: approval rate per life situation and answer length.
  • Calibration: 50 answers hand-labelled before launch; rubric re-weighted after 100 and 300 onboarded.

Why the contrast group matters

If women without the signals stay as well as the signal group, paid can buy broadly. If they drop off, the signals are the filter plan B must find in paid traffic.

Plan B · the $50,000 scale test

What you'll see, and what you don't see, when volume goes ten times up.

What you'll see

  • Meta (UK, US, women 39-55, creative self-selects), paid newsletters and creators (the paid twin of the community route), Google Search, Apple Search Ads after launch, Nordics capped.
  • Three stages: $12k calibrate four channels, $15k triple the best two, $18k the best one at ten times plan A's run-rate. $5k holdout and creative refresh.
  • The winning hooks and first frames from plan A, judged on day 7, not clicks.
  • Every visitor still answers the same four open questions.

What you don't see

  • The cost curve: cost per onboarding and per day-7 active against daily spend, per channel, three levels.
  • The ceiling: not the census (US pool is not exhausted within $50k; the Nordics are). The limit is the in-market slice and creative fatigue.
  • Early warning: the share of signal-group applicants per channel. When it drops, the channel is running out of the right women, days before day-7 data.
  • Stop and scale rules: next stage only if cost per day-7 active and signal share hold within 25%; stop at +50% stage on stage.
  • The honest line: on public benchmarks paid does not pay back in year 1. The test is whether AxisX's intent-selected women convert 2 to 4 times above them.
