AxisX, The Founding 500: appendix. The numbers, explained.

How to read this appendix. Written the way an analytics coach would walk a founder through it: first the answer, then the sum, then the decision it changes on Monday. Every number comes from the brief, from a public source with the URL next to it, or is labelled as an assumption to be measured on the first 500. The deck keeps one idea per slide; everything behind each slide is here.

Panel validation. Four review rounds by a simulated panel of leading practitioners (AI role-play on their public work; names are in the work log, not here) checked these details: the questions to the client (round 1), the full brief and the economic anchor (round 2), the campaign, the private founder invites and the success metrics with stop rules (round 3), and plan A as a whole (round 4). Changes the panel forced: a contrast group next to the selected group, a sourced free-to-paid anchor (2.1 to 6.2 percent instead of an unsourced 10 to 30 percent), community leads chosen on public role rather than on health posts, one ID from quiz to day 7, and the founder's hours counted as a cost. Where a number is still an assumption, it says so.

    3. Plan B: stages and the ceiling

    Plan B: the $50,000 scale test (16:27)

    Model: data/plan_b_model.py (output in plan_b_uitkomst.txt). Sources: onderzoek-paid.md, induik 2-6.

    The honest answer first

    On public benchmarks paid acquisition does not pay back in year 1. Blended cost ~$23 per completed onboarding needs ~21% of onboarded women to pay at £100 (30% at the £70 founding price). Health & Fitness benchmark: 2.9% median, 6.2% top quartile download-to-paid (RevenueCat 2026). So the $50k does not buy growth; it buys the answer to two questions:

    1. Do our onboarded, intent-selected women convert far above download benchmarks? (lever 1: conversion)

    2. How far can cost per onboarding fall, and where does it rise? (lever 2: the cost curve and the ceiling)

    Not a bigger plan A: no founder Q&As, no personal invites (they don't scale and don't count).

    Starts only when

  • Plan A delivered: step rates per source, D7 cohort 1 vs cohort 2, intent profile, founding-price yes %.
  • App live in the App Store (Google App campaigns and Apple Search Ads need a store app; TestFlight has no SKAN attribution).
  • Meta data-source category checked in Events Manager (health & wellness restrictions).

    The ceiling: what saturates first

    1. Right women, not all women: the in-market slice (recognises perimenopause, wants help) is unknown; illustratively 10% of the iOS pool saturates the UK at ~$19k and the Nordics at ~$11k (induik 3, assumption).

    2. Early warning: the share of signal-group applicants per channel (intent profile from plan A). When it drops, the channel is running out of the right women, days before D7 shows it.

    3. Creative fatigue: frequency above ~3 per week (practitioner rule, unverified); 8 openings refreshed every stage.

    4. Q4 CPMs (Superads: monthly CPM swings), launch lands in Nov-Dec.

    Stop and scale rules

  • Scale a channel to the next stage only if cost per D7-active and signal-group share hold within 25% of the previous stage (our rule).
  • Stop a channel when cost per D7-active rises more than 50% stage on stage, or signal-group share falls below cohort-2 levels.

    • What goes in the investor deck as proof

  • Cost curve per channel: cost per onboarding and per D7-active against daily spend, three stages.
  • Signal-group share per channel (quality at day 0).
  • D7 cohort 1 vs cohort 2 from plan A (how retention falls when selection loosens).
  • Founding-price yes % and, after launch, real paid conversion of onboarded women.
  • The break-even line: required conversion at each cost level. Scalable = the cost curve stays under that line for at least one channel.

    9. DECISION 16:22 (Bjorn): open questions + AI classification (replaces the closed quiz in section 3; section 5 rules become the classifier output)

    Principle: she tells her own story, the same way the app works. AI reads it, labels it, and the selection runs on signals in her own words, not on tick boxes.

    9.1 What she sees (4 open questions + 5 hard facts)

    Open (free text, voice input allowed):

    1. "Tell it like you'd tell a friend: what's changed lately?"

    2. "What have you already tried to make sense of it?"

    3. "What would make the next two weeks worth it for you?"

    4. "What's going on in your life right now that any plan has to fit around?"

    Hard facts (closed, needed for eligibility): age band · iPhone yes/no · "2 minutes a day for 14 days?" · email · consent (health data, separate box) · founding price yes/no.

    The contrast group is the test: if they retain as well as the signal group, our signals don't matter and paid can buy broadly; if they retain much worse, the signals are the filter plan B must find in paid traffic.

    9.4 Calibration

  • Before go-live: the founder and one team member label the same 50 answers by hand; AI vs human agreement must reach 80% per signal (our rule) or the rubric is rewritten.
  • After 100 onboarded: per signal, compare D7 open and Active founder D7 (with vs without signal). Keep signals with clear lift, drop the rest, re-weight. Repeat at 300.
  • Output: a validated "intent profile" (which words and actions predict day 7 in this audience).

    • 9.5 Technical set-up

    1. Quiz in web2wave with open text fields; on submit a webhook sends answers + user_id to the AxisX backend.

    2. Backend calls an LLM with structured output (fixed JSON schema), temperature 0, prompt and model version stored with every result.

    3. Labels + score go to the application table (sheet/CRM) next to email, invite code and hook tags. Raw text stays in the health-data store, not in marketing tools.

    4. Rules engine: signal group -> auto-approve within quota; contrast group -> random draw; "later" and borderline cases -> human review queue (founder/team), never an automatic rejection.

    5. Approved -> TestFlight email invite -> in-app login with the same email -> events (onboarding_complete, app_open, feedback_given, report_pdf_created) keyed on user_id.

    6. Weekly join: labels x events = lift table per signal, per community, per hook.

    Cost: covered by the $400 AI-quiz token line in plan A (about 4,000 applicants, short texts); check against current model pricing before launch.

    9.6 GDPR / trust

  • Free text about symptoms is health data (GDPR art. 9): explicit consent naming both purposes (building her preview, selecting founding members).
  • No solely automated rejection (art. 22): every "later" and borderline decision is checked by a person; she can ask for review.
  • LLM provider under a data processing agreement, no training on her data; EU/UK processing where available.
  • Retention: applicants not admitted -> raw text deleted after 30 days, only aggregated labels kept. Admitted -> text becomes part of her own app system, under the app's privacy terms.
  • Never to ad platforms: no raw text, no labels, no quiz events with health meaning.
  • Bias check weekly: approval rate per life_context tag and per answer length; if short or non-native writers are approved less, fix the rubric.

    • 9.7 Why this matters for plan B

    The intent profile is a day-0 quality signal per channel. In the $50k test we see within days, not weeks, whether a paid channel sends signal-group women or contrast-group women, before any D7 data exists. That makes saturation visible early: when a channel's share of signal-group applicants drops, the channel is running out of the right women.

    10. DECISION 16:25 (Bjorn): founding price for EVERY applicant

    Everyone who applies to the Founders Circle (admitted now, cohort 2, or "later") gets a founding price on the full app at launch: [£X a year instead of £100, proposal £70 = 30% off, to be set by the founder].

  • Trigger to apply: value even if she is not admitted now, so no applicant leaves empty-handed (also protects against the negativity the founder fears).
  • Copy (landing + application): "Apply and your founding price is locked in, whether you join now or at launch."
  • Turns the application list into a launch list with a price reason to come back on release day.
  • Pay signal stays: "Lock in my founding price" (yes/no, no payment) is the measure of willingness to pay, per group and per channel.
  • Economics: at £70 the net per payer is £70 x 0.85 = £59.50 (vs £85), so break-even per onboarding is 30% lower. Plan B models both prices; the founding price applies only to founding applicants, not to paid-acquired users later.

DOEL_ONBOARDED = 500          # brief

    ("landing -> quiz start", 0.50, "Adapty web-to-app: 40-60%"),

def funnel():
    tot = 1.0
    for naam, r, bron in STAPPEN:
        tot *= r
    return tot


def plan_a():
    conv = funnel()
    k = 0.20                    # K-factor-benchmark >0,20 'zeer goed' (blogbronnen, onderzoek-concurrentie-ai.md)
    via_referral = DOEL_ONBOARDED * k

    bezoekers = top / conv

    print(f"  landing -> onboarding complete: {conv:.1%}")
    print(f"  onboarded via referral (K=0.20): {via_referral:.0f}; via funnel: {top:.0f}")
    print(f"  landing-bezoekers nodig: {bezoekers:,.0f}")
