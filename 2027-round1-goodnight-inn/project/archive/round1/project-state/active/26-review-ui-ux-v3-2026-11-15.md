## Findings in detail

### UX3-01 · End labels not at their values (S2, chart clarity)

**Where.** Cumulative cash chart of What waits:
- 09 (Expected): `texts/09` lines 127-128 give "B +$6.8M" and "A +$6.8M".
- 10 (Low): "A −$4.6M", "B −$5.8M".
- 11 (High): "A +$19.2M", "B +$19.7M".

**What I see.**
- In 09, A and B end at the same value, so B's line lies under A's. The label "B +$6.8M" is pushed down onto the
  $0M gridline. The gridline even breaks around the label.
- In 10, "A −$4.6M" sits on the $0M line.
- In 11, A's label sits above B's although B ends higher: +$19.7M against +$19.2M.

**Why it matters.** This is the one chart where the picture and its labels disagree. A reader who trusts the position
reads "B ends at zero" in Expected and "A ends at zero" in Low. The title claims "B ends within $1M of A". The point is
right, but the labels' layout undercuts it.

**Suggestion.** When the two ends are within a label's height, write one label: "A and B +$6.8M". Otherwise put each
label at its own value, in value order. Effort S.

### UX3-02 · Build's bars hide the biggest loss (S2, chart clarity)

**Where.** 19, the plan with eight moves.

**What I see.** The bars are sorted highest NPV first. From eight moves on, the list scrolls (scrollbar at the card's
right edge). The bar that falls below the fold is the most negative one: Energy programme, −$12.2M. That is the last
item of the visual's alt text (`texts/19` line 1247). The total card says −$21.2M; the visible bars add up to much
less.

**Why it matters.** The reader who builds the largest plan is the reader most likely to ask "what is dragging me
down?", and the answer is hidden.

**Suggestion.** Sort by size of effect, so the largest bars, plus or minus, stay in view. Or lower the row height so
ten rows fit. Effort S.

### UX3-03 · Page 2 at laptop width (S2, hierarchy)

**Where.** 22: a 1366 x 768 window, where Power BI fits the page at 64 %.

**What I see.** The page stays whole and nothing breaks. But the dense parts fall to roughly 8 px of text height in
the capture: the tracking table under "Our recommendation", the hero table's column labels, the tranche lines and the
chart caption. Body text elsewhere is about 9 px.

**Why it matters.** Many judges score on a laptop. Page 1 survives the scaling better because its cards carry less
text.

**Suggestion.** Two options:
- After the submission: move the tracking table into a tooltip or a fourth tab.
- Now: nothing. The full-screen button in Power BI's bar (22, bottom right) is the reader's own fix.

Effort M to L. **Leave for this round.**

### UX3-04 · Column labels that read as one phrase (S3, chart clarity)

**Where.** 05 zoom left, the hero table head: "test 1 NPV through 2032". The same in Build (16): "NPV A year by 2032".

**What I see.** "test 1" is the label of the year-on-cost value column, but it sits flush against "NPV through 2032",
the next column's title. In Build, "NPV" and "A year by 2032" have one space between them.

**Suggestion.** Move "test 1" and "test 2" up to the line with "8%: our cost of money" and "pays back by 2032 →",
where the tests are explained, or widen the gap. Effort S.

### UX3-05 · Navy has three meanings, and the refresh changes colour (S3, colour)

**Where.** 05: the hero table bar of "Refresh, 10 oldest hotels" is navy, the colour of the two "2028 gate" extensions.
In the plan bar below, tranche 3 (the refresh) is slate and tranche 2 is navy. Navy is also B's line in What waits (09)
and "You" in Build (17).

**Why it matters.** One colour should mean one thing. Here the refresh is one colour in one chart and another colour in
the next.

**Suggestion.** Give the refresh the plan bar's slate in the hero table too. Leave B and You as they are: they never
meet tranche 2 on one view. Effort S.

### UX3-06 · Forward and back in different places (S3, wayfinding)

**Where.**
- 01: "Next: what we fund, and why →", a filled navy pill in the footer, bottom right.
- 05: "← The Brand Review", an outline pill in the header, between the eyebrow and the tabs.

The footer of page 2 holds only Source and Definitions.

**Why it matters.** It hardly matters. The back control is visible and labelled, and Power BI's own "2 of 2" arrows
help too (22). A reader who looks for back where forward was finds nothing there for a moment.

**Suggestion.** None for this round. If the header were redone: put both page buttons in the same corner of the
header or of the footer. Effort S.

### UX3-07 · Tooltip leftovers (S3, tooltips)

**Where and what.**
- 12, 13, 14: the package tooltip is sized for eight moves. With three moves, its lower panel is about one third
  empty white.
- 08 and 15: the year tooltip's last row reads "−$24.7M none yet" and "−$30.1M none yet". The C value and the
  "Your plan" value run together as one phrase.

**Suggestion.** Write "Your plan: not built" in the grey caption style, or a dash. Leave the height: it is
Power BI's fixed tooltip size. Effort S.

### UX3-08 · Ranges without a name (S3, copy)

**Where.**
- 09: B's numbers end with "−$6.9M to +$12.6M".
- 16: under "Your plan", "−$5.5M to +$12.7M"; also 17, 19 and 20.

Nothing says these are the Low-to-High NPV range.

**Suggestion.** Prefix "Low to High:". Effort S.

### UX3-09 · F4's bands and dark dots go unexplained (S3, chart clarity)

**Where.** 01, F4.

**What I see.** The dots are in three bands: highway, city and airport (`texts/01`: y about 1, 2 and 3 per dot). The
bands touch, so the plot reads as one cloud. The diamonds sit on their band's row and match the "Airport ◆ 82.0%"
labels to the left, but only an attentive reader will see that. The seven dark, named dots are the seven fullest
hotels. The title implies this; nothing on the chart says it.

**Suggestion.** A hairline or a little more space between the bands, and "● the seven fullest" beside the band labels.
Effort M. Leave.

### UX3-10 to UX3-17 · Polish

- **UX3-10** (07): Kiosks' NPV in High prints "+$0.03M" (`texts/07` line 63). Every other value has one decimal. It
  is honest (it is not zero), but it catches the eye.
- **UX3-11** (05): "The app carries it: +$3.4M of the +$3.3M" (`texts/05` line 122). The kiosks' −$0.6M explains it,
  but a reader stops at a part that is bigger than its whole. "+$3.4M on its own; the package nets +$3.3M" reads
  without the stop.
- **UX3-12** (09, 16): the scatter card's title stands 11 px lower than the title of the card beside it. On
  Recommended (05) the two card titles share a line.
- **UX3-13** (01, zoom right): F2's "27%" sits directly above "2025" and reads as a two-line tick.
- **UX3-14** (20): over the budget, the scatter shows no "You". The plan card explains why, but the chart does not.
- **UX3-15** (`texts/01`, `texts/16`): the bars and dots carry their raw values as accessible names
  ("6.7547715999111535", "(9400000, 14366891.399429627)"). The visual's own alt text comes first and says the same in
  plain words, so a screen-reader user is not lost, only slowed. This comes from Power BI. Leave.
- **UX3-16** (16, 20): the zero line of Build's bars runs down past the last bar to the card's bottom.
- **UX3-17** (05 vs 09, 16): on Recommended, "ⓘ Hover a year" sits inside the plot, on the gridlines. In What waits
  and Build it sits in the card's top-right corner like every other chip.
